# Buying a mutual fund from zero

A mutual fund pools money from many investors. A licensed asset management company (AMC) invests it, and a trustee holds the assets. You own **units**, valued at the daily NAV. You do not need a broker.

Read guides/start-here first. Background: funds/fund-types, funds/reading-nav, funds/mufap.

## Step 1: Choose the fund type
Match the fund to your goal and horizon (details in funds/fund-types):
| Need | Fund type |
|---|---|
| Emergency fund, cash parking | Money market |
| 1 to 3 years, low risk | Sovereign income or income |
| Moderate risk, one-stop | Balanced, asset allocation, fund of funds |
| 5+ years growth | Equity or index fund / ETF |
| Retirement with tax credit | VPS pension fund |
| Gold exposure | Commodity (gold) fund, where available |

Each fund has an SECP risk profile (very low to very high). Pick one that matches your own risk profile.

## Step 2: Compare funds
1. Compare only funds in the **same category** and same Shariah/conventional type.
2. Look at returns over 1 and 3 years, not just 30 days. Ask Igent to rank funds (query_navs uses MUFAP data).
3. Check loads (front-end and back-end) and the expense ratio (TER) in the Fund Manager Report (FMR).
4. Check fund size (AUM). Very small funds can be merged or closed.
5. For debt funds, check credit quality and maturity in the FMR.
6. For equity funds, compare with KSE-100 (or KMI-30 for Islamic funds) over several years.
7. Check the AMC is licensed by SECP and a MUFAP member.
See funds/reading-nav for the maths, including how loads change the real return.

## Step 3: Open an account directly with the AMC
Most AMCs offer online account opening on their website or app.

Documents (typical):
- CNIC (NICOP for overseas Pakistanis).
- Bank account (IBAN) in your own name.
- Mobile number and email in your own name.
- Source of income details.
- Filer status / NTN, if you have it.
- Zakat exemption declaration (CZ-50), only if claiming exemption.
- Nominee details, if offered.

Steps:
1. Download the AMC's official app or visit its official website (check the link from MUFAP or SECP).
2. Fill the account form and complete KYC (biometric or video verification, document upload).
3. Complete the **risk profile questionnaire** honestly. SECP rules require the AMC to assess suitability. If you choose a riskier fund than your profile, you may have to sign an acknowledgement.
4. Read the fund's offering document and key fact statement.
5. Some AMCs offer simplified accounts with lighter documentation for smaller investments. Ask the AMC about current options and their limits.

## Step 4: Invest
1. Select the fund and enter the amount.
2. Pay by bank transfer, online banking, or the payment method shown in the AMC app. Pay only into the **fund's or AMC's official collection account**, never into a person's account.
3. **Cut-off time:** each AMC sets a daily cut-off. Requests received before it get that day's NAV. Requests after it get the next working day's NAV. This is called forward pricing.
4. Units are issued at the **offer price** (NAV plus any front-end load).
5. You get a confirmation by email/SMS showing units and price.

Example (made-up numbers): you invest Rs 50,000. NAV is Rs 100 and there is no load. You get 500 units. If NAV later rises to Rs 110, your holding is worth Rs 55,000 before tax.

## Step 5: Set up a systematic investment plan (SIP)
- Many AMCs let you invest a fixed amount every month by standing instruction or auto-debit.
- Choose the date (just after payday works well) and the amount.
- You can usually pause or stop it any time. Check the AMC's terms.
- SIPs suit equity and balanced funds best, because they smooth out entry prices.

## Step 6: Redeem (sell units)
1. Submit a redemption request in the app or by form.
2. You get the **redemption price** (NAV minus any back-end load) for the day your request is accepted under the cut-off rule.
3. Money is paid to your registered bank account. Money market funds usually pay fastest (often within one or two working days). Other funds can take a few working days. Some AMCs offer instant redemption up to a limit.
4. The AMC deducts capital gains tax on any gain.
5. Check for back-end loads on fixed-term or capital-protected plans before redeeming early.

## Step 7: Tax
- Dividends from the fund are taxed at source.
- Redemption gains have CGT deducted by the AMC.
- Rates depend on filer status and change each budget. See basics/tax.
- Ask the AMC for an annual tax certificate. Use it in your return and wealth statement (guides/become-a-filer).

## Step 8: Statements and tracking
- AMCs send periodic account statements by email and show holdings in the app.
- Check each statement against your own records.
- Add your holdings to Igent's My Money so Igent can value them daily with MUFAP NAVs.
- Read the monthly FMR for big changes in the fund's holdings or manager.

## Shariah-compliant funds
- Most categories have Islamic versions: Islamic money market, income, asset allocation, equity, VPS and ETFs.
- They invest in sukuk, Islamic bank deposits and Shariah-screened shares, under a Shariah adviser.
- Purification is done inside the fund, so you usually do nothing extra (see shariah/screening).
- Compare Islamic funds with Islamic peers and with KMI-30 for equity.

## VPS pension funds
- The Voluntary Pension System lets you save for retirement in sub-funds (equity, debt, money market, and Shariah versions).
- Contributions can earn an **income tax credit**, within limits set by law. Check current rules with FBR or the AMC.
- Money is meant to stay until retirement age. Early withdrawal can be taxed.
- You choose an allocation scheme (higher or lower risk) and can usually change it.
- At retirement, rules allow part withdrawal and part as an income plan. Check the AMC's current terms.
- Useful for salaried people and self-employed who pay income tax.

## Red flags
- An "agent" asks you to pay cash or into a personal account.
- Promised fixed returns from a fund that is not a fixed-return plan.
- A fund or AMC you cannot find on MUFAP or SECP.
- Pressure to switch funds often (you may pay loads each time).
- Choosing a fund only by its last 30-day annualised return.
