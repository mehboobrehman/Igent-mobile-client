# Asset Management Companies (AMCs)

An **asset management company (AMC)** is a company licensed by SECP to set up and run mutual funds (collective investment schemes). Many also offer investment advisory services and run pension funds. The AMC collects money from many investors, invests it under each fund's stated objective, and issues units to investors at the daily NAV.

For fund categories see funds/fund-types. For NAV, loads and TER maths see funds/reading-nav. For the buying process see guides/mutual-funds-step-by-step.

## The legal framework
- **SECP** licenses and supervises AMCs. AMCs are non-banking finance companies (NBFCs).
- The **Non-Banking Finance Companies (Establishment and Regulation) Rules, 2003** set out how NBFCs, including AMCs, are formed and licensed.
- The **Non-Banking Finance Companies and Notified Entities Regulations, 2008** cover operations. "Notified entities" include the funds themselves. These Regulations hold the detailed fund rules (investment limits, fees, disclosures).
- Both are amended from time to time. The current text is on secp.gov.pk under Laws.
- **MUFAP** is the industry association, not the regulator (see funds/mufap).

## Three parties in every fund
| Party | Role |
|---|---|
| AMC (management company) | Makes investment decisions, prices units, runs sales and service |
| Trustee | Holds the fund's assets in trust for unit holders, oversees the AMC |
| Unit holders (you) | Own the fund's net assets in proportion to units held |

## Why your money sits with the trustee
- The trustee holds the fund's assets in a trust on behalf of investors and works to protect their rights. It often also acts as custodian of the securities.
- The **Central Depository Company (CDC)** and **Digital Custodian Company (DCC)** act as trustee for most funds. The trustee of each fund is named in its offering document and in MUFAP's fund directory.
- Fund assets are kept separate from the AMC's own money. The AMC manages the fund but does not own its assets. If the AMC itself has money trouble, fund assets are not the AMC's property.
- This is why you should pay only into the **fund's or trustee's official collection account**, never into an AMC employee's or agent's personal account.
- Segregation does not protect you from market losses. If the fund's investments fall, your NAV falls.

## Key fund documents
- **Trust deed:** the founding document, signed between the AMC and the trustee. It sets out how the fund is run. It is a public document.
- **Offering document (OD):** an SECP-approved document. It covers investment policy, risk profile, fees and charges, trustee and auditor, and the procedures for purchase, redemption and conversion. Supplements update it. Read the latest version on the AMC's website.
- **Key fact statement:** a short summary of the fund's main features and costs.
- **Fund Manager Report (FMR):** published monthly on the AMC website. It gives a synopsis of each fund's performance in the previous month.
- **Annual and quarterly financial reports:** audited accounts of each fund.

## What to read in an FMR
- Returns versus the benchmark over several periods.
- Asset allocation and top holdings. Look for concentration in one stock, sector or issuer.
- For debt funds: credit quality mix and weighted average maturity.
- Fund size (AUM) and its trend.
- TER, management fee and loads.
- Risk profile and any fund stability rating.
- Changes in fund manager or investment committee.

## Fees and costs
- **Management fee:** charged by the AMC as a yearly % of net assets. It is deducted inside the NAV daily.
- **Total expense ratio (TER):** all fund running costs as a % of average net assets. That includes management fee, sales tax on the fee, trustee fee, SECP fee and other costs.
- **Front-end load:** charged when you buy. Often reduced or waived when investing directly online.
- **Back-end load:** charged when you exit, mostly in plans with a lock-in period.
- **Sales tax:** charged on the management fee and included in the TER.

Fee levels vary by fund and change over time. SECP sets caps. Check the fund's OD, latest FMR and MUFAP's expense ratio and load pages for live values. Do not quote remembered figures.

## Ratings: PACRA and VIS
Two credit rating agencies, **PACRA** and **VIS**, rate AMCs and funds.
- **Asset manager (management quality) rating:** an opinion on the AMC's expertise, governance, investment process, risk management, customer service and operations. It is not a credit rating and does not predict fund returns. VIS uses a scale from AM1 (excellent management characteristics) down to AM5 (weak), with + and ++ steps in the middle grades.
- **Fund stability rating:** given mainly to money market and income funds. It shows how stable the NAV is expected to be, based on credit, liquidity and market risk. The scale runs from AAA(f), the highest stability, down to B(f). The "(f)" marks it as a fund rating, not a company credit rating.
- **Fund performance rankings** (e.g. VIS star rankings) are based on past returns only.

Ratings change. Read the current rating and report on pacra.com or vis.com.pk, or in the FMR.

## How to check an AMC is licensed
1. Look it up on secp.gov.pk under licensed NBFCs (asset management services).
2. Check MUFAP's members list and fund directory on mufap.com.pk.
3. Get the app or website link from SECP or MUFAP, not from a WhatsApp message or ad.
MUFAP's member list changes over time. Read it live rather than relying on a remembered list.

## Account, plans and switching
- **Open an account** directly with the AMC online, or through its distributors, often banks. MUFAP lists AMC distributors and sales staff. Steps and documents are in guides/mutual-funds-step-by-step.
- **Investment plans / SIP:** a fixed monthly amount by auto-debit or standing instruction.
- **Conversion (switching):** moving units from one fund to another fund of the **same AMC**. It is treated as a redemption from one fund and a purchase in the other, so loads and capital gains tax can apply. Check the OD.
- **Redemption timing:** requests before the cut-off get that day's price (forward pricing). Payment goes to your registered bank account within the time the OD and SECP rules allow. Money market funds usually pay fastest.

## Reading your account statement
Check each statement for:
- Your name, CNIC, account (folio) number and registered bank account.
- Each transaction: date, fund, units, price (NAV, offer or redemption) and amount.
- Loads deducted, and tax (CGT or dividend tax) withheld.
- Dividends paid out or reinvested as extra units.
- Closing units and value at the latest NAV.
Report any transaction you did not make to the AMC at once.

## Complaints
1. Complain to the AMC in writing (email, app or complaint form). Get a reference number.
2. If unresolved, escalate to SECP's **Service Desk Management System (SDMS)**, its online complaint and query portal. Reach it through secp.gov.pk. You can track status by SMS and email.
3. MUFAP's website also has a complaint portal link.
General steps and other channels are in guides/complaints-and-safety.

## Red flags
- **"Guaranteed" or fixed high returns** from anything other than a fixed-return plan described in an SECP-approved OD.
- **Unlicensed "investment companies"**, modarabas or apps that take deposits and promise monthly profit. Check SECP and MUFAP first.
- **Agents asking for cash**, or for payment into a personal bank account or wallet.
- No offering document, no FMR, or a fund missing from MUFAP's NAV list.
- Pressure to switch funds often, which earns the seller loads.

Rules and fees change. Confirm with SECP, MUFAP or the AMC's latest documents, or a licensed adviser. This note is general information, not legal or tax advice.
