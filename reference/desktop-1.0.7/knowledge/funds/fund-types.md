# Fund categories

Asset management companies (AMCs) run Pakistani mutual funds under SECP's NBFC Regulations. SECP defines the categories. Each fund or plan carries a **risk profile** (very low to very high) under SECP's suitability and risk categorisation rules (Circular 06 of 2022), and AMCs must assess investor suitability. Most categories exist in both **conventional** and **Shariah-compliant** versions. Shariah funds invest in sukuk, Islamic bank deposits and Shariah-screened shares, under a Shariah adviser.

## Categories (typical risk and use)
| Category | Invests in | Typical risk | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Money market | T-bills, bank placements, very short paper (short weighted maturity) | Very low / low | Cash parking, emergency fund. Yield tracks the policy rate. |
| Sovereign income (govt securities) | T-bills, PIBs, GoP Ijara sukuk | Low | Near-safe, more rate-sensitive than money market |
| Income | TFCs, sukuk, govt paper, deposits; longer duration | Low–medium | Slightly higher yield. NAV moves with rates. |
| Aggressive (fixed) income | Lower-rated or longer debt | Medium | Yield seekers. Credit risk. |
| Fixed-rate/return and capital-protected plans | Term plans held to maturity | Low | Locking in a rate. Exit penalties. |
| Balanced | Mix of equity and debt within set bands | Medium–high | One-stop moderate risk |
| Asset allocation | Flexible equity/debt mix chosen by manager | Medium–high | Manager timing |
| Fund of funds | Other mutual funds, often in risk-profiled plans | Varies | Ready-made allocation, extra fee layer |
| Equity | Mainly listed shares (high minimum equity exposure) | High | Long-term growth, 5+ years |
| Index tracker / ETF | Replicates an index (see funds/etfs) | High | Low-cost market exposure |
| Commodity (gold) | Gold-linked exposure | High | Gold exposure without storage |
| VPS pension funds | Sub-funds: equity, debt, money market (and Shariah versions) | Varies by allocation | Retirement saving with tax credit |

SECP rules also allow "investment plans" inside a fund (for example fixed-return, capital-protected, fund-of-funds and allocation plans). Category rules can be revised; check SECP and MUFAP for the current list.

## Notes per type
- **Money market funds** are the main "safe rate" proxy for a liquid investor. Redemption is usually paid within one or two working days. Some AMCs offer instant redemption up to a limit.
- **Income funds** gain NAV when rates fall and lose when rates rise (duration effect). Credit events (TFC defaults) have historically caused NAV drops in aggressive income funds.
- **Equity funds** should be compared with the KSE-100 total return (or KMI-30 for Shariah funds) after fees.
- **VPS (Voluntary Pension System):** contributions get an income tax credit under section 63 of the Income Tax Ordinance, within limits set by law. Money is meant to stay invested until retirement age, and tax applies on early withdrawal. Allocation schemes range from high to low volatility.
- **Tax:** fund dividends are taxed at source (often with a higher rate on the debt-income part than the equity part), and redemption gains pay CGT. Rates change each budget; see basics/tax.

## Matching funds to goals
- Need within 1 year: money market.
- 1–3 years: money market, sovereign income or income.
- 5+ years with tolerance for drawdowns: equity or index, maybe through balanced or asset allocation funds.
- Religious preference: use the Islamic version in the same category and compare returns within Islamic peers.

## Pitfalls
- Comparing an income fund's annualised daily return with an equity fund's 1-year return is apples vs oranges.
- High past returns in debt funds mostly reflect a high policy rate at the time. They fall when rates fall. Check the current policy rate with query_rates.
- Check fund size, TER and loads. Very small funds can be merged or wound up.
