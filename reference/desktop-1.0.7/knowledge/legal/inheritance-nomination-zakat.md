# Nominees, inheritance and zakat

**General information only, not legal advice.** Laws and procedures change, and each institution has its own forms. Confirm with the institution, the official source or a licensed lawyer. For inheritance shares, wills and zakat rulings, consult a qualified scholar or lawyer.

## Nominee vs legal heir
- A **nominee** is the person you name on an account or certificate to receive the money when you die.
- A **legal heir** is a person entitled to a share of your estate under the personal law of inheritance that applies to you.
- Pakistani courts have held many times that a nomination is **not a gift and not a will**. The nominee generally collects the money **as a trustee for all legal heirs**. They must pass on each heir's share. This has been applied, for example, to National Savings certificates.
- Practice differs by product. Some institutions pay the nominee directly for small amounts against an undertaking to share with the heirs. Others need a succession certificate. Check the rules of your bank, AMC, broker, CDNS or insurer.
- Always name a nominee and keep it up to date. It makes the claim faster. It does not decide who finally inherits.

## What happens after death: transmission
"Transmission" means moving a deceased person's holdings to the heirs. Each institution has its own claim form, but the pattern is similar.

| Asset | Usual route |
|---|---|
| Shares in CDC (broker sub-account or Investor Account) | Contact the broker or CDC. CDC generally needs a succession certificate or letter of administration before it moves the shares to the heirs' own CDC accounts. |
| Mutual fund and VPS units | Claim with the AMC or pension fund manager. They may pay or transfer units to heirs, based on a succession certificate and their own claim form. |
| Bank accounts and deposits | Inform the bank. It freezes sole accounts on notice of death and pays out according to a succession certificate and its own policy. |
| National Savings certificates and accounts | Claim at the CDNS centre. CDNS pays legal heirs in line with a succession certificate. For small amounts, it may pay the nominee against an undertaking to share with heirs. See guides/national-savings-step-by-step. |

### Two ways to get a succession certificate
1. **Court route:** apply to a civil court under the Succession Act, 1925. This route is needed where there is a dispute.
2. **NADRA route:** under the Letters of Administration and Succession Certificates Act, 2020 (Islamabad Capital Territory), NADRA can issue a letter of administration or succession certificate through its Succession Facilitation Unit. It uses NADRA's family registration record. Provinces have passed similar laws. Check which route covers your area.
   - One heir can apply for all, with written authority from the others.
   - NADRA publishes a public notice and invites objections. If none come, it takes biometric verification of all heirs. The certificate shows each heir's share under their personal law.
   - NADRA must send the case back to the court route if there is a "factual controversy". This includes objections, disputes about who the heirs are, or **any heir being a minor**.

### Documents typically needed
- Death certificate (NADRA computerised).
- CNICs of all legal heirs, and the NADRA family registration certificate.
- Authority from the other heirs, if one heir applies.
- Details of the assets: account numbers, CDC account IDs, fund folio numbers, certificate numbers, and recent statements.
- The institution's own claim form, and any indemnity or affidavit it asks for.

## Joint holdings
- Bank accounts can be opened jointly with operating instructions such as "either or survivor" or "jointly". Whether the survivor can keep operating after a death depends on the account terms and bank policy.
- Being the survivor on an account does not by itself settle who inherits the money. The deceased's share may still be part of the estate. Ask the bank and a lawyer.
- Joint holdings in funds, CDC accounts and certificates follow the institution's rules. Read them when you open the account.

## Minors
- Children can usually hold bank accounts, fund units and some certificates through a **guardian**, who operates the account until the child comes of age.
- When a minor inherits, courts usually supervise. The Guardians and Wards Act, 1890 governs court-appointed guardians of a minor's property. The NADRA succession route is not available when an heir is a minor.

## Keep a list for your family
Many families lose time, or even money, because nobody knows what the deceased owned. Keep an up-to-date list in a safe place, and tell a trusted family member where it is:
- Each bank, broker, AMC, insurer and CDNS holding, with account or folio numbers.
- Broker name and CDC account ID; NCCPL UIN.
- Nominees on each holding.
- Loans you gave or owe, gold, property papers, the location of documents.
- Contact numbers of the institutions.

Do not write passwords or PINs on this list. Igent's My Money can hold a summary of what you own and where. See guides/complaints-and-safety for which records to keep.

## Wills (wasiyyat), in concept
- Under the generally applied rules of Islamic law in Pakistan, a Muslim's will (wasiyyat) is limited to **one-third** of the estate left after funeral costs and debts.
- A bequest to a legal heir is generally not valid unless the other heirs consent after the death.
- Schools of fiqh differ on details. The rest of the estate passes by fixed inheritance shares.
- A nomination cannot be used to get around inheritance shares.
- Non-Muslims follow their own personal law.
- Consult a qualified scholar and a lawyer to write a valid will. Keep it where your heirs can find it.

## Zakat on investments
**Compulsory deduction.** The Zakat and Ushr Ordinance, 1980 lets banks and other institutions deduct zakat at source. The deduction is on the **Valuation Date, the first day of Ramadan** (the start of the zakat year). It applies to Muslim citizens of Pakistan, and to companies majority-owned by them.

The Ordinance's First Schedule lists the assets, including:
- savings bank and similar accounts;
- notice deposits;
- fixed deposit receipts and term certificates;
- savings and deposit certificates, such as National Savings certificates;
- NIT units and ICP mutual fund certificates;
- government securities;
- shares and debentures of companies;
- annuities, life insurance policies and provident funds.

How a specific product is treated depends on the product and the institution. Check its terms or ask it.

**Exemption by declaration.** A person can avoid compulsory deduction by filing a sworn declaration with each deducting institution. It is commonly known as **Form CZ-50**. It must be filed a set period before the Valuation Date.
- **Who can file:** a Muslim who states which recognised fiqh they follow, and that their faith and fiqh do not oblige them to pay zakat in the way the Ordinance lays down.
- **How:** it is sworn before an oath commissioner, notary or magistrate, with two witnesses.
- **How long it lasts:** a filed declaration stays valid while it and the asset remain with that institution. Give a fresh copy for each new account.
- If zakat was deducted despite a valid declaration, the law allows a refund claim in the prescribed manner. Ask your institution.

Non-Muslims are outside compulsory zakat. Tell the institution your religion at account opening.

**No amounts here.** The nisab (minimum balance below which no deduction is made) is notified fresh each zakat year, and rates and rules can change. Check the official announcement before Ramadan. SBP publishes the nisab for bank accounts in a circular. Some provinces also have their own zakat laws.

Paying zakat yourself on wealth that is not deducted at source is a personal religious matter. Ask a qualified scholar how to calculate it on shares, funds, gold and cash.
