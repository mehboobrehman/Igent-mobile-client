# Islamic banking in Pakistan

Islamic banks avoid interest (riba). Instead of lending money for a fixed return, they share profit and loss, trade, or lease assets. They are licensed and supervised by the State Bank of Pakistan (SBP), like conventional banks. How Islamic deposit rates relate to conventional rates, and the SBP rule on the minimum share of pool income paid to savers, are in savings/bank-deposits.

Rules change. Confirm with SBP, the bank, or a qualified scholar or adviser. This is not religious, legal or tax advice.

## Who offers Islamic banking
SBP calls them Islamic Banking Institutions (IBIs). There are three forms:
- **Full-fledged Islamic bank**: the whole bank works on Islamic terms.
- **Islamic banking subsidiary** of a conventional bank.
- **Islamic banking division of a conventional bank**: it runs separate Islamic banking branches (often called "Islamic windows"). Its funds and pools are kept separate from the conventional side.

Before choosing, check that the bank or branch is SBP-licensed for Islamic banking (sbp.org.pk).

## Shariah governance
SBP's **Shariah Governance Framework** applies to all IBIs:
- **Shariah Board (SB)**: Shariah scholars appointed by the bank's board of directors. Every product needs SB approval. SB rulings are binding on the bank.
- **Resident Shariah Board Member**: an SB member who handles day-to-day Shariah questions.
- **Shariah Compliance Department**: checks that products, documents and processes follow SB rulings.
- **Internal Shariah audit** and **external Shariah audit**: independent reviews of compliance.
- **SBP's own Shariah Board**: its rulings, and the AAOIFI Shariah standards adopted by SBP, form part of the rules IBIs must follow.

Some scholars and customers still debate whether certain products are truly different from interest. Users who care should read the bank's Shariah Board report and ask their own scholar.

## Common modes of Islamic finance
| Mode | How it works | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Mudarabah** | One side gives money (rabb-ul-maal), the other manages it (mudarib). Profit is shared by a pre-agreed ratio. Financial loss falls on the money provider, unless caused by the manager's negligence or misconduct. | Savings and term deposits |
| **Musharakah** | A partnership. All partners put in capital. Profit is shared by agreement. Loss is shared in proportion to capital. | Business finance, some deposit pools |
| **Diminishing Musharakah** | The bank and customer jointly own an asset. The customer pays rent on the bank's share and buys that share bit by bit until owning it all. | Home finance, car finance |
| **Murabaha** | The bank buys a good and sells it to the customer at cost plus a disclosed profit, paid later. | Trade and working capital finance |
| **Ijarah** | A lease. The bank owns the asset and rents it out. The bank keeps ownership risk. | Car leasing, equipment |
| **Istisna** | An order to manufacture or build something, with price fixed in advance. | Construction, manufacturing finance |
| **Salam** | Advance payment for goods delivered later, with full price paid up front. | Agriculture, commodities |
| **Sukuk** | Certificates of ownership in an asset or project, paying a share of its income (often rent). | Government and corporate fund-raising |

The Government of Pakistan issues Ijarah sukuk, auctioned through SBP. Islamic banks and Islamic funds hold them as their main "safe" asset, much like conventional banks hold T-bills and PIBs.

## How Islamic deposit profit works
Most Islamic savings and term deposits are **Mudarabah**:
1. The bank puts depositors' money in one or more **pools**. Each pool works like a separate business with its own assets, income and expenses.
2. Before each month starts, the bank announces the **profit sharing ratio (PSR)** between depositors and the bank, and the **weightages** for each deposit category. Weightages decide who gets a bigger share: larger balances and longer tenors usually get higher weightages.
3. At month end, the bank works out the pool's actual income, deducts direct expenses, and splits the profit by the PSR.
4. The depositors' share is divided across deposit categories by weightage. This gives each category's profit rate for that month.
5. The bank publishes the PSR, weightages and actual rates paid. Look on its website or branch notice board.

Points to know:
- The rate quoted in advance is an **expected** rate. The actual rate depends on pool income.
- Banks may smooth payouts by keeping a profit equalisation reserve, or by giving up part of their own share (hiba) in a weak month. SBP rules limit these practices.
- In theory, depositors share losses. In practice, pool assets are mostly government sukuk and secured financing, so losses on deposit pools are rare. Still, the return is not contractually fixed.
- A PSR agreed when you open a term deposit cannot be cut downward during its tenor.

## Islamic vs conventional deposits for an investor
| Point | Conventional | Islamic |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | Interest (loan to the bank) | Profit sharing (Mudarabah investment) |
| Rate | Stated rate | Expected rate, actual declared monthly |
| SBP minimum rule | Linked to SBP floor rate | Minimum share of pool yield |
| Current accounts | No profit | No profit |
| Deposit protection | DPC member banks | Same DPC cover; Islamic and conventional balances at one bank are added together |
| Tax and zakat | Withholding tax; zakat may be deducted | Same treatment |

How to compare:
- Compare an Islamic bank's actual paid rates over the past months, not only its advertised rate.
- Compare Islamic banks with each other, and with Islamic money market and income funds (funds/fund-types).
- Compare after tax (basics/tax) and against inflation.
- Islamic rates usually move with general interest rates, because pool assets are priced off market benchmarks. They are not immune to rate cuts.

Example (made-up numbers): a pool earns Rs 1,000,000 net in a month. PSR is 50:50. Depositors get Rs 500,000, shared across categories by weightage. A category with weightage 1.2 earns 1.2 times the rate of a category with weightage 1.0.

## Riba judgment and the move to interest-free banking
- Pakistan's **Federal Shariat Court** has ruled that interest (riba) in all its forms is against Shariah. It declared the interest-based banking system and related laws un-Islamic and directed the government to move the whole financial system to an interest-free basis within a set period.
- The Constitution's **Article 38(f)**, a principle of policy, commits the state to eliminate riba. It was later amended to include a target date for this "as far as practicable".
- The government and SBP have stated they are working on the transition, including converting conventional banking and government debt to Shariah-compliant forms.
- Timelines, legal appeals and progress can change. **Check SBP (sbp.org.pk) for the current status.** Do not assume conventional products will disappear on a fixed date.
