## The Big Idea

To buy shares on PSX you need two things: a trading account with a **licensed broker** and a **CDC account** where your shares live. Opening them is mostly online paperwork. The real skill is doing it safely: checking the broker is genuine, paying only into official accounts, and turning on alerts so nobody can touch your shares without you knowing.

## Explain It Like I'm New

### Step 1: Pick a licensed broker

A **broker** is a firm that places your orders on PSX. In Pakistan it must be licensed by SECP and hold a PSX trading right (it is called a **TREC holder**, from Trading Rights Entitlement Certificate).

How to check a broker is real:

1. Find its name on the PSX website's list of brokers / TREC holders.
2. Find it on SECP's list of licensed securities brokers at secp.gov.pk.
3. Make sure the app or website you use really belongs to that broker. Download apps only from links on the broker's official site.
4. Search the news for complaints or penalties against it.

Then compare (Igent does not recommend any broker): the full fee schedule, an app that keeps working on busy days, online opening and support, and any inactivity or custody fees.

### Step 2: Gather your documents

Your broker will give you the exact list. It usually includes:

- Your valid **CNIC** (NICOP for overseas Pakistanis).
- A bank account in your own name, shown by its **IBAN**. All money goes in from and comes out to this account.
- Proof of income or source of funds, such as a salary slip or business proof.
- Your NTN / filer status (from the Beginner level).
- A Zakat exemption declaration (CZ-50), only if you want to claim exemption.
- Nominee details, if offered.
- A mobile number and email registered in your own name.

### Step 3: Complete KYC

**KYC (Know Your Customer)** is the check every regulated firm must do to confirm who you are and where your money comes from. Expect biometric or video verification, a selfie or document upload, and a **risk profile questionnaire**. Answer honestly. The questions exist to protect you from products that do not suit you.

Read the account agreement, especially the parts on fees, margin and trading authority. **Never sign a blanket authority letting the broker trade for you.**

### Step 4: Your CDC sub-account and UIN

Once approved, the broker opens a **CDC sub-account** in your name. Your shares are held there, separate from the broker's own shares. **NCCPL** registers you with a **UIN (Unique Identification Number)**, usually based on your CNIC. You should get SMS or email confirmations from CDC and NCCPL. If you do not, ask the broker why.

Another route is a **CDC Investor Account**, opened directly with CDC. It keeps shares outside any broker (safer), but selling is slower.

### Step 5: Turn on alerts and CDC Access

This is the step most people skip, and it is the most important one.

1. Make sure CDC has **your own** mobile number and email, not a broker employee's or agent's.
2. Turn on CDC SMS and e-alerts. Every movement in your shares gets reported to you.
3. Register for CDC's online access service so you can see your holdings yourself. Check cdcpakistan.com for current steps.

### Step 6: Fund the account

1. Get the broker's **client account** details from its official website or app.
2. Transfer from your own bank account by online banking, IBFT or cheque.
3. **Never** pay into an employee's personal account or a mobile wallet. Never hand over cash.
4. Keep the receipt and check the money shows up in your trading account.

### Red flags

| Red flag | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| "Send money to my personal account" | Possible fraud. Real brokers use official client accounts. |
| "I'll trade for you, we share the profit" | Unauthorised, risky, often a scam. |
| Broker or app not on PSX/SECP lists | Do not use it. |
| Guaranteed returns or "insider" tips | No real investment guarantees high returns. |
| CDC alerts suddenly stop, or your mobile was changed | Contact CDC and the broker immediately. |

Problems? Complain to the broker in writing, then escalate to PSX and SECP.

## A Pakistani Example

Ayesha is a teacher in Multan. She has her emergency fund in a money market fund and wants to start with shares using Rs 50,000.

- She finds her chosen broker on both the PSX and SECP lists and reads its fee schedule.
- She uploads her CNIC and salary slip, does video KYC, and answers the risk questions honestly.
- A few days later CDC and NCCPL send SMS confirmations of her sub-account and UIN.
- She registers her own number for CDC alerts and transfers Rs 50,000 from her own bank account to the broker's client account, saving the receipt.

A week later a "senior trader" messages her on WhatsApp offering to "double her money" if she sends Rs 20,000 to his JazzCash number. Ayesha knows the red flags. She blocks him and reports the number.

## Try It

Before opening a real account, practise the routine. Visit the PSX website and find its list of brokers, then find SECP's list of licensed brokers, and check that one broker you have heard of appears on both. Meanwhile, use Igent's Practice tab, a paper-trading portfolio with Rs 1,000,000 of pretend money, to get used to placing orders without any real money at risk.

## Common Mistakes

- Downloading a "broker app" from an ad or a WhatsApp link instead of the broker's official website.
- Letting a broker employee register their own phone number on your CDC account.
- Signing a trading authority without reading it.

## Key Takeaways

- Use only a broker on both the PSX and SECP lists.
- Your shares live in a CDC account in your name, and NCCPL gives you a UIN.
- KYC and the risk questionnaire are there to protect you. Answer honestly.
- Turn on CDC alerts with your own number and check your holdings every month.
- Money goes only from your own bank account to the broker's official client account.
