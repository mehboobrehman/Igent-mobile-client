## The Big Idea

Many Pakistani investors want their money to follow Islamic principles: no **riba** (interest), no forbidden businesses, and no excessive uncertainty. The good news is that a full halal path exists, from Islamic bank deposits and sukuk to screened shares and Islamic funds. You just need to know how screening works and where to check. Igent is not a Shariah authority, so for religious questions follow your own scholar.

## Explain It Like I'm New

### How stocks are screened

PSX publishes a list of **Shariah-compliant companies**. The screening method needs SECP approval and is revised from time to time. It works in two steps.

**Step 1: Business screen.** The company's main business must be permissible. This rules out, for example, conventional banks, conventional insurance and leasing, alcohol, gambling and other forbidden activities. Conventional banks are never compliant. Islamic banks can be.

**Step 2: Financial ratio screens.** A company with a halal business must also pass money tests:

| Test | The idea in plain words |
|---|---|
| Interest-bearing debt vs total assets | Must stay below a limit. The company can't lean heavily on conventional loans. |
| Non-compliant investments vs total assets | Must stay below a limit. |
| Non-compliant income vs total revenue | Must be very small, such as a little interest income. |
| Illiquid assets vs total assets | Must be above a minimum, so the share represents a real business, not just cash. |
| Share price vs net liquid assets per share | The price must be at least the net liquid assets per share. |

The exact limits are set in the current methodology and have changed over time. Check PSX before quoting a number.

Status can change at each review, for example if a company takes big conventional loans. Re-check every new list.

### KMI-30 and KMI All Share

| Index | What it contains | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **PSX-KMI All Share** | Every company on the Shariah-compliant list. | Your universe: the stocks you may choose from. |
| **KMI-30** | The 30 most liquid compliant companies, with a cap on any single company's weight. | Your benchmark, to judge how you are doing. |

A compliant small company can be in KMI All Share but not KMI-30, which only takes the most traded names.

You can see members on the PSX Data Portal's indices section, or ask Igent's Chat.

### Purification of dividends

Even a compliant company may earn a tiny amount of non-compliant income, such as some bank interest. Scholars ask investors to give that share of their **dividends** to charity. This is called **purification**. It is not Zakat; it is separate.

**Purification amount = dividend received × (non-compliant income ÷ total revenue)**

Many scholars do not require purification of capital gains on compliant shares, but views differ, so follow your scholar.

### Islamic funds: the easy route

Most fund categories exist in Islamic versions: Islamic money market, Islamic income, Islamic equity, Islamic VPS pension funds, and ETFs that track Shariah indices. Each fund works under a **Shariah adviser** who approves what it holds. Islamic funds purify inside the fund, so you normally do nothing extra. Compare an Islamic fund with other Islamic funds in its category, and an Islamic equity fund with the KMI-30.

### Islamic banking and sukuk, briefly

**Islamic banks** avoid interest. Their savings accounts usually work on **Mudarabah**: you provide money, the bank manages it, and profit is shared by a pre-agreed ratio. Your return is a share of actual pool profit, not a fixed interest rate. Islamic banks are licensed and supervised by the State Bank of Pakistan (SBP), and each has a Shariah Board.

**Sukuk** are certificates that give you ownership in an asset or project and pay a share of its income, often rent. The Government of Pakistan issues **Ijarah sukuk**, which are the main "safe" asset for Islamic banks and Islamic funds, a bit like T-bills for conventional ones.

A complete halal portfolio might look like this (illustrative):

| Basket | Halal option |
|---|---|
| Emergency fund | Islamic bank savings account or Islamic money market fund |
| Fixed income | Islamic income funds, government Ijarah sukuk |
| Shares | Islamic equity fund, KMI-30 based ETF, or stocks from KMI All Share |
| Gold | Physical gold, bought the way your scholar advises |

## A Pakistani Example

Maryam, a doctor in Peshawar, wants a fully halal portfolio. She holds 300 shares of a cement company, say Lucky Cement, which she found on the latest KMI All Share list. All numbers are made up.

The company pays Rs 20 per share. Her gross dividend: 300 × 20 = Rs 6,000. After an example 15% withholding tax she receives Rs 5,100.

An Islamic fund's monthly report shows a purification ratio of 1% for this company. Following her scholar's guidance, she applies it to her dividend:

**Purification = 6,000 × 0.01 = Rs 60** given to charity.

Later a new PSX list drops one of her other holdings because its debt rose above the limit. Maryam asks her scholar how to exit and plans a calm sale. For the rest of her savings she uses Islamic funds, which purify for her.

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming a company is halal because its business sounds fine. The ratio tests matter too.
- Using an old compliant list. Companies join and leave at each review.
- Confusing purification with Zakat. They are separate.
- Judging an Islamic equity fund against the KSE-100 instead of the KMI-30.
- Treating Igent or any app as a religious authority. Follow your own scholar.

## Key Takeaways

- PSX screens companies by business and by financial ratios, under an SECP-approved method that can change.
- KMI All Share is the universe; KMI-30 is the benchmark.
- Purify the non-compliant share of dividends to charity; Islamic funds do this for you.
- Islamic banks, sukuk and Islamic funds give halal options for every basket.
- Status can change, so check the latest PSX list every time.
