## The Big Idea

Many Pakistani families trust gold and US dollars more than anything else, and there is a reason: when the rupee falls, both tend to rise in rupee terms. That makes them useful **hedges**, a kind of insurance. But they pay no profit, they can fall too, and buying them carries hidden costs. Use them as a slice of your savings, not the whole plan.

## Explain It Like I'm New

### Gold basics: tola, karat and sarafa rates

- A **tola** is the traditional unit for gold in Pakistan. **1 tola = 11.6638 grams.** Prices are quoted per tola and per 10 grams.
- **Karat** measures purity. **24K** is the purest commercial gold. **22K** is 22/24 pure, and jewellery is often 22K or lower.
- The **sarafa rate** is the daily gold rate announced by the All Pakistan Gems and Jewellers Sarafa Association. It is a guide. Each dealer's actual buy and sell quotes can differ.

Quick check: if 24K is Rs 240,000 per tola (an example), the gold in 22K is worth about 240,000 x 22/24 = **Rs 220,000** per tola.

### The hidden costs: making charges and buy-back

When you buy **jewellery**, you pay:
- the gold value,
- **making charges** (the jeweller's fee for crafting it),
- sales tax where it applies.

When you sell it back, the jeweller usually pays only the gold value, and often deducts a percentage too. So jewellery often has to rise a fair amount just to break even. **Bars and coins** have smaller costs, which is why investors prefer them. **Gold funds** run by AMCs (where available) let you own gold without storage worries, though they have fees.

### Why gold and dollars are hedges

The local gold price is roughly:

> world gold price (in dollars) x dollar rate (rupees per dollar) + a local premium or discount

So if the rupee weakens, local gold rises even if world gold does not move. A US dollar note works the same way: when the rupee falls, each dollar is worth more rupees.

That is why people buy them in uncertain times. Pakistan's rupee has gone through long calm periods followed by sharp falls, so this protection has been valuable before.

### Why they are not magic

- **No income.** Gold and cash dollars pay no profit. A safe rupee option pays profit every year.
- **They can fall.** World gold has had long periods of falling or going nowhere. The rupee can also stay stable, or even strengthen, for long stretches.
- **Costs and safety.** Making charges, dealer spreads, theft risk and storage all eat into returns.
- **Late buying.** People often rush in after a big rise, when the price is already high.

So compare them with the after-tax safe rate. If the rupee is stable and safe options pay well, cash dollars at home can quietly lose to a simple savings scheme.

### Two dollar rates

You will see two different dollar prices:

| Rate | Who uses it | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Interbank rate** | Banks, importers, exporters | SBP publishes a daily weighted-average closing rate. This is the official reference rate. |
| **Open-market rate** | Individuals buying cash dollars from licensed exchange companies | Usually a little above interbank, with a buy and a sell price |

A small gap between the two is normal. A **wide gap** is a warning sign of dollar shortage or stress in the market. Igent's Dashboard shows the open-market dollar rate (the sell side). Buy dollars only from licensed exchange companies or banks, never from informal dealers.

## A Pakistani Example

Ayesha's mother wants to buy a 1-tola 22K gold chain as savings. Using made-up numbers:

- Gold value: Rs 220,000. Making charges: Rs 10,000. Total paid: **Rs 230,000**.
- A year later, the rupee has weakened and the gold value of the chain has risen to Rs 240,000.
- The jeweller deducts 5% on buy-back: 240,000 x 0.95 = **Rs 228,000**.

Gold went up, yet she would get back Rs 2,000 less than she paid. Making charges and the deduction ate the gain. If she had bought a gold bar or used a gold fund instead, more of the rise would have reached her.

Meanwhile, Bilal kept Rs 300,000 worth of cash dollars at home for two years while the rupee stayed calm. His dollars barely changed in rupee terms and earned nothing. A safe rupee option paying profit would have done better in those two years. In a year when the rupee falls sharply, the story would reverse. That is what a hedge is: protection for bad times, not a money machine.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating jewellery as an investment without counting making charges and buy-back deductions.
- Comparing a 22K jewellery price with the 24K rate.
- Believing gold "always goes up".
- Keeping all savings in gold or dollars and earning no profit at all.
- Buying from unlicensed dealers or joining "gold committees" that promise fixed monthly returns.

## Key Takeaways

- 1 tola = 11.6638 grams. 22K is 22/24 as pure as 24K.
- Local gold moves with world gold and the dollar rate, so it rises when the rupee falls.
- Gold and dollars are hedges against rupee weakness, not guaranteed profit.
- Jewellery has hidden costs. Bars and gold funds are cheaper ways to own gold.
- The interbank rate is the official reference. The open-market rate is what you pay for cash dollars.
