## The Big Idea

In investing, there is no free lunch. If you want the chance of a higher **return** (the profit you earn), you must accept more **risk** (the chance of losing money or of things going differently than you hoped). Understanding this trade-off, and how time changes it, protects you from both fear and scams.

## Explain It Like I'm New

### The trade-off

Think of it as a ladder. The higher you climb, the better the view, but the harder a fall can be.

| Option | Typical risk | Return potential | Can the value drop? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bank savings, National Savings | Very low | Low | No, your rupees are safe (but inflation still bites) |
| Money market funds | Very low to low | Low | Rarely, and only a little |
| Income funds | Low to medium | Medium | Yes, small moves |
| Gold | Medium to high | Medium | Yes, can fall for years |
| Equity (share) funds | High | High over long periods | Yes, can fall 20-30% or more in a bad year |
| A single company's shares | Very high | High, but very uneven | Yes, can fall a lot or even go to near zero |

Nobody can promise you the return at the top of the ladder. You can only expect it on average, over many years, with bumps along the way.

### Volatility vs permanent loss

These two are very different, and mixing them up causes many beginner mistakes.

**Volatility** means prices going up and down. A good equity fund might be up 25% one year and down 15% the next. It feels scary, but if the things the fund owns are still good businesses, prices often recover over time. A drop you do not sell into is a loss "on paper".

**Permanent loss** means the money is truly gone. This happens when:
- a company fails or a scheme was a fraud,
- you put everything in one bet and it collapses,
- you are **forced to sell** during a fall because you needed the money.

That last one is important. Volatility becomes a permanent loss when you sell at the bottom. That is why the emergency fund from the last lesson matters so much.

### The maths of falling

Losses hurt more than gains help. If something worth Rs 100 falls 50% to Rs 50, it must rise **100%** to get back to Rs 100. A 30% fall needs about a 43% rise to recover. This is why avoiding big permanent losses matters more than chasing the highest return.

### Time horizon

Your **time horizon** is how long until you need the money. It decides how much risk you can take.

| Need the money in | What fits |
|---|---|
| Under 1 year | Safe options only |
| 1 to 3 years | Mostly safe and low-risk options |
| 3 to 5 years | A blend, mostly low-risk with a little growth |
| 5+ years | Growth options like equity funds make sense alongside safe ones |

Over a short time, share prices can do almost anything. Over many years, the ups and downs tend to smooth out, and growth has more time to show. That is a tendency, not a guarantee.

### Risk capacity vs risk tolerance

**Risk capacity** is how much loss you can afford, based on income, dependants and horizon. **Risk tolerance** is how much loss you can stomach without panicking. Use the lower of the two.

### The biggest red flag: "guaranteed high return"

If anyone promises a **high** return that is also **guaranteed**, stop. That breaks the basic rule of risk and return. Safe options pay modest returns. High returns always carry risk.

Typical scam lines:
- "Earn 10% profit every month, guaranteed."
- "Double your money in 6 months, no risk."
- "Join early, and earn more for each friend you bring."

The last one is a **Ponzi** or pyramid scheme: early members are paid with money from new members, until it collapses. Check that any company is licensed by SECP (or SBP for banks) before giving it a rupee.

## A Pakistani Example

A man in Ayesha's neighbourhood runs a "business" that promises 10% profit every month. Her uncle wants to invest Rs 200,000.

Ayesha does the maths. 10% a month, compounded for 12 months, is 1.10^12 = about 3.14. That means Rs 200,000 would become about **Rs 628,000** in one year, a return of over 200% a year, "guaranteed". Compare that with safe options paying a modest yearly rate. No real business can promise that. The first few months of "profit" are often paid from new people's money, and then the organiser disappears.

Meanwhile, her cousin Bilal invests Rs 200,000 in an equity fund for 10 years. In year 2 it falls 20%, to about Rs 160,000 on paper. He does not need the money, so he waits instead of selling. The fall was volatility, not a permanent loss. Over the full 10 years, the fund's value depends on the market, and he knew going in that it could be up or down in any single year.

## Try It

Open the **compound calculator** below this lesson. Enter Rs 100,000 at 1% a month for 12 months, then at 10% a month. See how absurd the second number is. That is how you sniff out a scam: run the "guaranteed" rate through the calculator and ask whether any honest business could pay it.

## Common Mistakes

- Believing any "guaranteed" high-return offer, especially from a friend or relative.
- Panic selling during a fall and turning volatility into a permanent loss.
- Putting money needed next year into shares.
- Putting all your money into one company or one scheme.
- Judging an investment by one great year and forgetting the bad years.

## Key Takeaways

- Higher possible return always comes with higher risk.
- Volatility is temporary ups and downs. Permanent loss is money truly gone.
- A 50% fall needs a 100% rise to recover, so avoid big losses.
- Your time horizon decides how much risk you can take.
- "Guaranteed high return" is a red flag. Always check SECP or SBP licensing.
