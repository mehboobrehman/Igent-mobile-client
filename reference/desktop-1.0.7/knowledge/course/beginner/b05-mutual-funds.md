## The Big Idea

A mutual fund lets many people pool their money so professionals can invest it for them. You get diversification and expert management even with a small amount. A **money market fund** is the gentlest type: low risk, easy to withdraw, and often a great first step beyond a bank account.

## Explain It Like I'm New

### How a mutual fund works

Imagine 1,000 friends each put money into one pot, managed by an expert. Each owns a share of the pot in proportion to what they put in. That is a mutual fund.

- The manager is an **AMC (Asset Management Company)**, a company licensed and regulated by **SECP (Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan)**.
- A separate **trustee** (usually a bank or the Central Depository Company) holds the fund's assets. The AMC manages the money but cannot simply walk away with it.
- You own **units** of the fund, a bit like slices of a cake.
- **MUFAP (Mutual Funds Association of Pakistan)** publishes every fund's daily prices and returns. Igent reads this data too.

### NAV: the price of one unit

**NAV (Net Asset Value) per unit** is the value of one unit. It is calculated every business day:

> NAV = (everything the fund owns - what it owes) / number of units

If you invest Rs 50,000 when the NAV is Rs 100, you get 500 units. If the NAV later rises to Rs 110, your 500 units are worth Rs 55,000.

Funds use **forward pricing**: each AMC has a daily **cut-off time**. If your request arrives before it, you get that day's NAV. After it, you get the next working day's NAV.

### The main fund types

| Type | Invests in | Risk | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Money market | T-bills, bank placements, very short loans | Very low to low | Emergency fund, parking cash |
| Sovereign income / income | Government bonds, other debt | Low to medium | 1 to 3 years |
| Balanced / asset allocation | A mix of shares and debt | Medium to high | One-stop moderate risk |
| Equity | Mainly shares on PSX | High | 5+ years of growth |
| Index fund / ETF | Copies a stock index | High | Low-cost market exposure |
| VPS pension | Sub-funds for retirement | Varies | Retirement (with a tax credit, within legal limits) |

Most types come in both **conventional** and **Shariah-compliant (Islamic)** versions. Every fund also carries an SECP **risk profile**, from very low to very high.

### Why money market funds are an easy start

A money market fund holds the same kind of safe paper as T-bills and bank placements, so it tracks the safe rate. But it is much easier than buying T-bills yourself:

- **Small amounts** are usually fine.
- **Easy exit:** redemption is usually paid within one or two working days, and some AMCs offer instant redemption up to a limit.
- **Usually no front-end load.**
- Returns are shown as **annualised** yearly rates, so you can compare them with bank and T-bill rates.

It is "safe-ish", not risk-free. It is not a bank deposit, its return is not guaranteed, and returns fall when interest rates fall. But for parking money, it is often an excellent choice.

### Costs: loads and expenses

- A **front-end load** is a one-time fee taken when you buy, mostly on equity and balanced funds. With a 2% load, Rs 100,000 buys only Rs 98,000 worth of units. It is often zero for money market funds and for buying online direct.
- A **back-end load** is a fee for exiting early from some plans.
- The **TER (Total Expense Ratio)** is the yearly running cost, already taken out of the published returns.

### How to buy online (the steps)

1. Pick an AMC that is licensed by SECP and listed on MUFAP. Use only its official app or website.
2. Open an account with your CNIC, a bank account in your own name, and your mobile and email. Complete **KYC** (identity checks).
3. Fill the **risk profile questionnaire** honestly.
4. Choose the fund, enter the amount, and pay only into the fund's **official** collection account, never a person's account.
5. You get a confirmation with your units and price. Track it in the app, and add it to Igent's My Money.

## A Pakistani Example

Bilal has just finished building his emergency fund of Rs 200,000 in a bank savings account. He moves Rs 150,000 of it to a money market fund and keeps Rs 50,000 in the bank for instant needs.

- NAV on the day: Rs 10 (made-up). He gets 15,000 units.
- Say the fund earns 11% a year (an example). After a year his units are worth about Rs 166,500 before tax.
- His bank savings rate was lower (say 9%, also an example), and he can still redeem within a working day or two.

His sister Ayesha wants a 10-year growth investment. She looks at an equity fund with a 2% front-end load. On Rs 100,000, Rs 2,000 goes to the load and Rs 98,000 is invested. She checks whether the same AMC waives the load for online buying.

## Common Mistakes

- Thinking a money market fund is a guaranteed bank deposit.
- Choosing a fund only by its last 30-day return. Look at longer periods.
- Comparing an equity fund with a money market fund. Compare funds within the same type.
- Paying a front-end load you could have avoided by buying online.
- Paying an "agent" in cash or into a personal account.

## Key Takeaways

- A mutual fund pools money, managed by an SECP-licensed AMC, with a trustee holding the assets.
- NAV is the price of one unit, calculated every business day.
- Fund types range from very low risk (money market) to high risk (equity).
- Money market funds are an easy, low-risk place to park money, but not guaranteed.
- Watch loads and expenses, and only use official AMC channels.
