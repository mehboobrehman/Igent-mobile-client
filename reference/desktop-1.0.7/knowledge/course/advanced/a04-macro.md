## The Big Idea

**Macro** means the big picture of the whole economy: interest rates, inflation, the rupee, government finances. You cannot control it, but it sets the weather for every investment you own. When the State Bank changes rates, your bank profit, your fund NAV and your share prices all feel it, just at different speeds. Understanding the chain helps you stay calm and make sensible moves.

## Explain It Like I'm New

### The policy rate: the master switch

The **State Bank of Pakistan (SBP)** has a **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)** that sets the **policy rate**, the base interest rate for the economy. SBP publishes the meeting calendar in advance. Each decision comes with a statement explaining why.

When the policy rate changes, it flows through like this:

| What | When rates are cut | When rates are raised |
|---|---|---|
| **KIBOR** and T-bill yields | Fall within days | Rise within days |
| Bank deposit and savings rates | Fall, with a lag | Rise, with a lag |
| Money market fund yields | Drift down | Drift up |
| Bond and income fund NAVs | Usually get a gain | Usually take a loss |
| Stocks overall | Usually helped | Usually hurt |
| Banks' margins | Tend to shrink over time | Tend to widen over time |

**KIBOR** (Karachi Interbank Offered Rate) is the rate banks charge each other. Many loans are priced off it.

Why do bond prices move opposite to rates? A bond pays fixed profit. If new bonds pay less after a cut, your older, higher-paying bond becomes more valuable, so its price rises. Longer bonds move more.

Why do stocks usually like cuts? Companies pay less interest, customers borrow more, and savers move money from deposits to shares because safe returns look less attractive.

### Inflation and the real rate

**Inflation** is how fast prices rise. The **Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS)** publishes the **CPI** (Consumer Price Index) monthly. The headline figure is **year-on-year (YoY)**: prices now versus the same month last year.

The **real rate** = interest rate − inflation. It tells you whether savers are actually gaining buying power.

SBP usually wants a clearly positive real rate. So when inflation falls fast and the real rate gets very high, rate cuts become more likely. When inflation jumps, cuts become less likely, or hikes return.

### The rupee, reserves and the IMF

The rupee's value against the dollar depends on oil imports, exports, remittances from overseas Pakistanis, and loans flowing in and out. **Foreign exchange reserves** are the dollars SBP holds. Low reserves make the rupee fragile.

Pakistan has often borrowed from the **IMF** (International Monetary Fund) under programmes with regular reviews. A successful review usually calms markets: the rupee firms, yields fall, stocks rise. A delay or breakdown can bring a sharp sell-off. IMF conditions, such as higher energy tariffs or new taxes, can still hurt particular sectors even when the deal helps the market overall.

### The federal budget

The government presents a budget each year, usually in June, for a tax year starting 1 July. Watch for changes to taxes on capital gains, dividends, bank profits and National Savings, plus sector duties. Proposals can change before the Finance Act passes, so wait for the final law.

### Expectations matter more than the event

Markets react to the **surprise**, not the news itself. If everyone expects a 1% cut and SBP cuts 1%, the market may barely move. A bigger or smaller move than expected is what shakes prices. Clues to expectations: T-bill yields before the meeting and brokerage polls. (Lesson a06 goes deeper.)

## A Pakistani Example

Use made-up round numbers, not today's figures.

- Policy rate: **12%**. CPI inflation: **8%**. Real policy rate = 12 − 8 = **+4%**. That is comfortably positive, so the market might expect cuts.
- You have **Rs 1,000,000** in a deposit paying 12%, and say tax on bank profit were 15% (an example rate). Profit before tax = Rs 120,000. After tax = Rs 102,000, or **10.2%**. Your real return after tax ≈ 10.2 − 8 = **+2.2%**.
- SBP then cuts by 2%. Over the following months, your deposit rate may fall towards 10%. An income fund holding government bonds may get a one-time NAV gain, then earn lower yields. Debt-heavy cement and auto shares may rally. Bank shares might lag.

None of this is guaranteed. A sudden oil shock or rupee slide could reverse the whole story.

## Common Mistakes

- Quoting an old interest rate or inflation figure. Always check the live tiles.
- Assuming a cutting cycle will continue forever. Cycles reverse.
- Forgetting that your deposit rate changes later than the policy rate.
- Reacting to an expected decision as if it were a surprise.
- Confusing the fiscal-year average inflation with the latest monthly YoY figure.

## Key Takeaways

- The SBP policy rate flows into KIBOR, deposits, funds and stocks at different speeds.
- Bond prices move opposite to rates. Stocks usually like cuts; banks are the exception.
- Real rate = interest rate − inflation. It shapes what SBP does next.
- IMF reviews, reserves and the budget can move the whole market.
