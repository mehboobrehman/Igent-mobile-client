## The Big Idea

You now have every piece: thesis, sizing, portfolio plan, sell rules and the safe-rate hurdle. This lesson puts them into one checklist you run before every decision, and a journal that turns your own wins and mistakes into lessons. This is how a beginner becomes an investor who decides for themselves.

## Explain It Like I'm New

### The full decision checklist

Pilots use checklists because stress makes everyone skip steps. Use this for every buy:

```text
FOUNDATION
[ ] 1. Emergency fund in place; expensive debt paid off
[ ] 2. Goal and horizon for this money: ______ (___ years)
[ ] 3. Fits my allocation plan? Core or satellite? Room within targets?
THE IDEA
[ ] 4. 5-line thesis written (expect, because, evidence, catalyst, wrong if)
[ ] 5. Evidence checked myself: results, valuation, sector, macro
[ ] 6. Is a fund a simpler way to get the same exposure?
THE NUMBERS
[ ] 7. After-tax safe rate for this horizon: ___%   Hurdle (+premium): ___%
[ ] 8. Good / normal / bad stories -> expected return ___%  Beats hurdle?
[ ] 9. Size = max loss / bad-case fall = Rs ___, adjusted for conviction,
       liquidity, existing holdings, and below my cap
[ ] 10. Buy plan: ___ now, rest in steps if thesis holds
THE EXIT
[ ] 11. Thesis-breakers written; price level that triggers a review: Rs ___
[ ] 12. Review dates: next result ______, calendar ______
[ ] 13. Journal entry written BEFORE placing the order
```

If any box fails, the answer is "not yet". "Not yet" is a perfectly good decision.

### Keeping an investment journal

An **investment journal** is a simple record of every decision and the reasons at the time. A notebook or spreadsheet is fine. Each entry:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Date, action, name | 10 Mar, Buy, XYZ |
| Price, amount, % of portfolio | Rs 150, Rs 15,000, 2.5% |
| Thesis (5 lines) | ... |
| Expected return vs hurdle | 12% vs 10.2% safe + 5 premium |
| How I feel | Excited after a strong week (careful!) |
| Review date and outcome | 10 Jun: thesis on track / broken / unclear |

Writing your feelings reveals patterns: many people find their worst trades came when they felt "sure" or "scared".

### Reviewing mistakes the right way

Separate **decision quality** from **outcome**. A good decision can lose money through bad luck. A bad decision can win by luck. Ask:

- Was my thesis right, wrong, or untested?
- Did I follow my own sizing and exit rules?
- Did I check the safe rate after tax?
- What would I do differently with only the information I had then?

### Your scorecard

Every few months, total up your closed decisions:

- **Hit rate:** how many beat the safe rate over the same period.
- **Average result vs the safe rate**, in percentage points.
- **Rule breaks:** how often you ignored your own checklist, and how those turned out.

Igent does the same for its own ideas. On **Idea Cards**, cards are scored against the safe rate (the 3-month T-bill rate when the card was made), and the header shows the hit rate and average points against the safe rate. Compare your scorecard with it honestly. If neither beats the safe rate over time, a simple fund-and-safe-options portfolio may be the smarter choice. That is a real, respectable answer.

### How to keep learning

- Read results and annual reports of companies you own, and ask what would break each new idea card.
- Use the **Glossary** whenever a term is unclear, and the **Wiki** for sector and macro background.
- SECP's investor education programme, **JamaPunji**, has free material and checklists.
- Re-read your journal every quarter. Your own mistakes are your best teacher.

## A Pakistani Example

*All numbers are made-up round figures for teaching, not real data or advice.*

Ayesha, 32, is a filer with a stable job. Her emergency fund is in a money market fund. She has **Rs 600,000** investable for at least 7 years. Her plan: 80% core (funds and National Savings), 20% satellite. Her satellite has Rs 90,000 used, so Rs 30,000 of room.

**Idea:** **Hub Power (HUBC)**, example price Rs 100, expected dividend Rs 10.

**Thesis (5 lines):** steady dividends from long-term power contracts; evidence from recent results and cash collections; catalyst is the annual result in two months; wrong if the dividend falls below Rs 6 or payments owed to the company keep piling up for two quarters.

**Hurdle:** 1-year safe option 12% (example), assumed 15% tax, so 10.2% after tax. Premium 4 points. Hurdle **14.2%**.

**Stories (after tax and costs):** good 30% chance at +35%; normal 50% at +14%; bad 20% at -20%.
Expected = 10.5 + 7 - 4 = **13.5%**. Just *below* her hurdle.

**Decision 1:** "Not yet." She adds HUBC to her Watchlist with a 5% alert, and writes a journal entry explaining why she passed.

Three weeks later the alert fires: the price fell to Rs 90 on market-wide selling. Company news has not changed. She reruns the numbers: the yield is now about 11%, and her stories become good +45%, normal +22%, bad -12%. Expected = 13.5 + 11 - 2.4 = **22.1%**, well above 14.2%.

**Size:** max loss 1.5% of Rs 600,000 = Rs 9,000. Bad-case fall for a really bad year: 30%. Size = 9,000 / 0.30 = **Rs 30,000**, which exactly fits her satellite room and is far below her cap. Medium conviction, so she starts with **Rs 15,000** (about 166 shares) and plans the rest after the annual result if the thesis holds.

**Exit plan:** review on result day; a 20% fall triggers a re-read; thesis-breakers as above. Journal entry written. Order placed.

Two months later the result shows the dividend intact, so she buys the second half. A year on, her scorecard records the outcome and whether she followed her rules.

## Common Mistakes

- **Skipping the checklist when excited.** That is exactly when you need it.
- **Writing the journal after the trade.** Memory rewrites reasons; write them first.
- **Judging by outcome only.** A lucky win can teach a bad habit.
- **Never scoring yourself against the safe rate.** Without a scorecard you cannot know if your stock-picking adds value.

## Key Takeaways

- One checklist covers foundation, idea, numbers and exit. A failed box means "not yet".
- Keep a journal with reasons and feelings, written before you act.
- Judge decision quality separately from outcome.
- Track your hit rate and average result against the safe rate.
- Use Chat, Idea Cards, Watchlist and Practice together, and keep learning. The decisions are now yours to make.
