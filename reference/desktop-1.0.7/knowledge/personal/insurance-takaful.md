# Insurance and takaful: what to buy and what to avoid

Insurance protects you against losses you cannot afford. It is a poor way to invest. Most money mistakes Pakistani savers make with insurance come from mixing the two.

This is general education, not personal financial, tax or legal advice. Rules change.

## Who regulates it
- **SECP** regulates insurance and takaful companies under the **Insurance Ordinance, 2000**.
- Takaful is governed by SECP's **Takaful Rules, 2012**. Conventional insurers can run Islamic business through a separate **window takaful** operation.
- Insurance disputes have a free ombudsman route (see Claims and complaints below). See legal/regulators-and-laws.

## Kinds of cover

| Type | What it does | Verdict for most people |
|---|---|---|
| **Term life** | Pays your family a sum if you die within the term. No payout if you survive. | Cheapest real protection. Buy if others depend on your income. |
| **Whole life / endowment** | Life cover plus a savings element paid at maturity. | Expensive cover with a low, opaque savings return. |
| **Unit-linked investment plan** | Part of each premium buys units in the insurer's funds; part pays for cover and charges. | Usually high costs. Compare hard against funds plus term cover. |
| **Health / hospitalisation** | Pays hospital bills up to set limits. Often via employer group cover. | Very useful. A big medical bill can wipe out savings. |
| **Motor** | Third-party and/or comprehensive cover for your vehicle. | Comprehensive is common for financed cars. |
| **Home / fire / property** | Covers building and contents against fire, theft and other named risks. | Worth it for valuable property. |
| **Travel** | Medical and trip cover abroad. Often needed for visas. | Buy when travelling. |

## How takaful works (basics)
- Participants contribute to a **Participants' Takaful Fund (PTF)**. Part of each contribution is a donation (**tabarru**) to a shared pool that pays members who suffer covered losses.
- The **takaful operator** manages the fund. It keeps its own shareholders' fund separate; the two must not be mixed.
- **Wakala model:** the operator acts as agent and takes a pre-agreed **wakala fee** for running the fund.
- **Mudarabah model:** the operator manages investments and shares the profit with participants at a pre-agreed ratio.
- Many operators combine both: wakala for running the pool, mudarabah for investing it.
- **Surplus:** if the PTF has money left after claims, reserves and costs, part of it may be shared with eligible participants. Surplus is not guaranteed.
- Takaful family plans can carry the same high charges as conventional unit-linked plans. "Islamic" does not mean low cost.

## Protection first
1. **Emergency fund** in a money market fund or bank savings (see funds/fund-types, savings/bank-deposits).
2. **Health cover** for you and your family.
3. **Term life** if anyone depends on your income. A common rule of thumb is cover of several years of income.
4. **Invest separately** for goals, using funds, NSS or shares.

"Buy term and invest the rest" is usually cheaper and more flexible than one policy doing both.

## Unit-linked plans and bancassurance mis-selling
Bancassurance means a bank sells an insurer's policy to its customers. The bank is the agent; the insurer carries the risk. The policy is not a bank deposit and the bank does not guarantee it.

SECP has received many complaints that bank staff sold insurance policies as "highly profitable investments" without explaining front-end charges. SECP has taken action against insurers, including ordering full premium refunds and clawback of bank commissions. It also issued the **Corporate Insurance Agents Regulations, 2020**, which require clear disclosure, after-sale call-back confirmation, and set minimum premium allocation rates.

Typical features to watch:
- **Low allocation in early years.** A large part of the first year's premium (and sometimes later years) may go to charges and commission instead of units.
- **Ongoing charges:** policy admin fee, mortality (cover) charge, fund management fee, and any bid-offer spread on units.
- **Surrender charges and lock-in.** Stopping in the first few years can return much less than you paid, sometimes almost nothing.
- **Long premium-paying term.** You commit to paying every year for many years.
- **Illustrated returns are not guaranteed.** Illustrations show projections at assumed growth rates. Actual fund returns can be lower.
- **"Profit rate" talk** at a bank counter often mixes up fund growth with a fixed deposit rate.

## Questions to ask before signing
- Is this insurance or a deposit? Who is the insurer?
- What percentage of my premium is invested each year, for every year?
- What are all the charges? Ask for them in writing.
- What do I get if I stop after 1, 2, 3 and 5 years?
- How many years must I keep paying?
- Are the illustrated values guaranteed? At what assumed return?
- What is the actual life cover amount, and what does term cover of the same amount cost?
- Which funds will my units go into, and what are their past returns after charges?

Do not rely on verbal promises. SECP advises that only what is written in the policy documents counts.

## Free-look period: how to cancel
- Most new life and family takaful policies have a **free-look period**: a short window after you receive the policy documents in which you can cancel and get your premium back (sometimes less medical exam costs).
- The length is set by the rules and the policy. Examples seen are 14 days, and some bank-sold plans state longer. **Check the free-look clause in your own policy document.**
- To cancel: write to the insurer (and the bank if sold there) within the window. Quote the policy number, ask for cancellation under the free-look clause, and keep proof of the date you sent it.
- After the window, cancelling is a **surrender**, and surrender charges apply.

## Claims and complaints
1. **Insurer first.** File the claim or complaint in writing with all documents. Keep copies and reference numbers.
2. **Federal Insurance Ombudsman (fio.gov.pk).** Free complaint route against insurers, under the Insurance Ordinance, 2000. Online complaint form and helpline are on its site.
3. **SECP SDMS (sdms.secp.gov.pk).** SECP's complaint portal for regulated entities, including insurers.

Full steps and escalation tips: guides/complaints-and-safety.

## How the agent should treat a user's existing policy
- **Never tell a user to just stop paying.** Surrendering early can lock in a large loss. Get the facts first.
- Ask for: policy type, insurer, annual premium, years paid, years left, sum assured, and the **current surrender value** (the insurer can provide it).
- **Separate the two parts.** The protection part is worth what equivalent term cover would cost. The investment part is the surrender value now plus future premiums.
- **Compare the future, not the past.** Premiums already paid are sunk. The question is: is paying the remaining premiums better than putting the same money into a fund or NSS plus buying term cover?
- Estimate the policy's return from here using the insurer's own illustration (treat it as optimistic) and compare it after tax with the safe rate (savings/safe-rates).
- Check whether the user is still inside the free-look period. If yes, that is the cheapest exit.
- Make sure the user keeps life and health cover in place before dropping any policy.
- If mis-selling seems likely, point to the complaint route above.
