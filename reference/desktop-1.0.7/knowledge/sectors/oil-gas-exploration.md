# Oil & Gas Exploration and Production (E&P)

E&P stocks have historically been large KSE-100 weights. They earn in effect in US dollars, since oil and gas prices are linked to international crude in USD. Their cash flows depend heavily on the gas **circular debt**.

## Example listed companies (tickers)
- **OGDC** (Oil & Gas Development Co.): state-controlled, historically the largest producer.
- **PPL** (Pakistan Petroleum): state-controlled, gas-heavy.
- **MARI** (Mari Energies): low-cost gas from the Mari field, with a growing exploration portfolio.
- **POL** (Pakistan Oilfields): Attock group, oil-weighted. Historically it has received cash more reliably than the state E&Ps.
- OGDC and PPL each hold a stake in the **Reko Diq** copper-gold project. This is a long-term value option.

## Earnings drivers
- **Oil price (Brent/Arab Light):** crude realisations and many gas prices are indexed to oil, often with caps or slabs in the pricing formula.
- **PKR/USD:** a weaker rupee raises rupee revenue. Live dollar rate: query_rates.
- **Production volumes:** fields decline naturally. Output is also curtailed when imported RLNG pushes local gas out of the system or when pipelines are damaged.
- **Exploration costs:** dry wells are written off, which hits quarterly EPS.

## Circular debt (key risk)
- Gas utilities (SNGPL, SSGC) do not pay E&Ps in full, so E&P receivables balloon. Profits look high but cash and dividends lag.
- The stock of circular debt includes principal and late-payment surcharges. It is large compared with the companies' market value.
- Governments regularly propose settlement plans (offsets, special dividends, write-offs). These often need IMF agreement and can affect minority shareholders.
- Clearing receivables is the main upside trigger for state E&Ps' dividends. Follow plans with search_news / read_news.

## Tax
- E&Ps pay corporate tax, super tax and royalties. Disputed super-tax provisions can be reversed later, which creates one-off profit jumps. Strip these out when comparing years.

## What to watch
- Brent and PKR. Higher oil lifts earnings, but also worsens the subsidy and circular-debt bill.
- IMF reviews and government circular-debt plans.
- Production data and new discoveries (PSX notices via query_announcements).
- Payout ratios and any special dividends tied to debt settlement.

## Pitfalls
- Do not value OGDC or PPL on P/E alone. Receivables quality and cash conversion matter more.
- One-off tax reversals can inflate reported profits.
- Geopolitical oil spikes can reverse within months.
