# Power Generation & Distribution

Listed power companies are mostly **independent power producers (IPPs)**. They sell electricity to the government buyer (CPPA-G) under long-term power purchase agreements (PPAs). **K-Electric** is the vertically integrated utility for Karachi.

## Example listed companies (tickers)
- **HUBC** (Hub Power Co.): historically the largest listed IPP, now also a holding company with coal, Thar mining and other interests. It has been diversifying, including into distribution.
- **KEL** (K-Electric): generation, transmission and distribution for Karachi. Its share price is low in rupee terms and it is heavily traded by retail investors.
- Others: e.g. **NPL** (Nishat Power), **NCPL** (Nishat Chunian Power), **LPL** (Lalpir), **KAPCO**. Many older plants have had PPAs expire or terminated. Check each company's current PPA status before analysing it.

## How IPPs earn (classic model)
- **Capacity payments:** fixed payments for being available, whether or not they are dispatched. These cover debt, fixed costs and a return on equity. Fuel costs are passed through.
- Under older contracts, returns were indexed to the USD (and US CPI), so PKR depreciation raised rupee earnings.
- The weakness is cash collection. CPPA-G pays late because of **power-sector circular debt**, so IPPs carry large receivables and dividends depend on cash received.

## Contract reset risk
- Installed capacity has grown far above average demand, so capacity payments became a political and IMF target.
- In 2024 the government terminated several IPP contracts (including Hubco's oil-fired plant) through negotiated settlements, and moved many others to revised terms (e.g. take-and-pay, lower returns). Terms vary by plant; verify each one.
- **K-Electric** depends on NEPRA decisions: multi-year tariff determinations, write-off claims and fuel cost adjustments. NEPRA rulings can move KEL sharply.

## Sensitivities
- **Policy and contract risk** is now the dominant factor, more than rates or PKR.
- **Interest rates:** late-payment income and financing costs both move with KIBOR. Live rates: query_rates.
- **PKR:** still matters where USD-indexed tariffs remain.
- **Fuel/LNG availability:** affects dispatch and fuel-cost pass-through, but usually not the capacity margin.
- **Circular debt clearance:** lump-sum government payments to IPPs can trigger special dividends.

## What to watch
- Government and IMF circular-debt plans, CPPA-G payment flows, and NEPRA tariff decisions.
- Privatisation of distribution companies (DISCOs).
- Dividends and ex-dates via query_announcements.

## Pitfalls
- High historical dividend yields on old IPPs can vanish when a PPA ends or is renegotiated. Always check the remaining PPA life.
- KEL's low share price does not make it cheap. Look at regulatory decisions and recovery rates.
- Reported profits may include accrued but uncollected receivables.
