# Fertilizer

Fertilizer has traditionally been a defensive, high-dividend sector. Urea producers get natural gas at regulated prices, usually below import parity, and have usually sold urea below international prices.

## Example listed companies (tickers)
- **FFC** (Fauji Fertilizer Company): large urea producer. It absorbed Fauji Fertilizer Bin Qasim (FFBL, a DAP producer) in a merger completed at the end of 2024, so it also makes DAP.
- **EFERT** (Engro Fertilizers): urea, plus imported and traded DAP/NPK.
- **FATIMA** (Fatima Fertilizer): urea, CAN, NP.
- **ENGROH** (Engro Holdings): a holding company with a stake in EFERT and other businesses.
- Some producers hold investments in other companies (e.g. FFC in a bank). These affect valuation. Check the latest annual report.

## Earnings drivers
- **Gas price and supply:** feed and fuel gas is the main cost. The government notifies gas prices, and plants on different networks (e.g. Mari field gas vs SNGPL/SSGC) pay different rates, so margins differ by company. OGRA and ECC notifications set the prices.
- **Urea price:** producers set prices themselves, usually in step with each other and often after gas price changes.
- **Offtake (sales volume):** depends on farm economics. Wheat support prices, crop returns and water availability matter most. Sales peak around the Rabi (wheat) and Kharif sowing seasons.
- **DAP:** imported or made from imported inputs, so it is sensitive to international prices and the PKR.
- **Inventory:** high urea stocks can lead to price discounts or requests for export permission.

## Data sources
- **NFDC** (National Fertilizer Development Centre) publishes monthly production, offtake and inventory.
- Price changes and results: search_news / read_news and query_announcements.

## Tax and policy
- Fertilizer makers pay corporate tax plus super tax. Budgets sometimes add sector-specific levies or excise. Check the latest Finance Act.
- Gas subsidy reform (often IMF-linked) is a recurring risk. Any move to unify or raise feed-gas prices hits margins unless it is passed on to farmers.

## What to watch
- Gas price notifications (OGRA/ECC), especially for feed gas.
- NFDC monthly offtake and inventory.
- Urea price announcements and any government talk of capping prices.
- International urea and DAP prices, which drive import parity and the case for exports.
- Dividend announcements: FFC and EFERT have traditionally been large payers.

## Pitfalls
- Regulated gas means policy risk: one cabinet decision can change margins overnight.
- High dividend yields partly reflect that risk.
- FFC figures after the merger are not comparable with its stand-alone history.
