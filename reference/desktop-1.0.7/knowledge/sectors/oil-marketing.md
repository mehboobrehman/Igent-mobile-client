# Oil Marketing Companies (OMCs)

OMCs import and buy fuel (petrol, diesel, furnace oil, jet fuel) and sell it through retail stations and to industry and power plants. The business is high volume and thin margin, and it ties up a lot of working capital.

## Example listed companies (tickers)
- **PSO** (Pakistan State Oil): state-owned and historically the market leader. It carries large receivables from the power and gas chain.
- **APL** (Attock Petroleum): Attock group, historically a cleaner balance sheet.
- **WAFI** (Wafi Energy Pakistan, formerly Shell Pakistan; confirm the ticker on PSX).
- **HASCOL** (Hascol Petroleum): has been financially distressed. Treat as speculative.
- Refineries (e.g. ATRL, NRL, PRL, CNERGY) are a related but separate sub-sector.

## How OMCs earn
- **Regulated margins:** the government fixes OMC and dealer margins per litre on petrol and diesel. Margins are revised only occasionally, so inflation can erode them for years. OGRA notifies changes.
- **Volumes:** **OCAC** (Oil Companies Advisory Council) data on monthly sales is widely reported in the press in the first days of each month.
- **Inventory gains and losses:** the government resets pump prices at regular intervals (historically every fortnight). When oil prices or the PKR move, OMCs book gains or losses on stock held. This often dominates quarterly profit.
- **Exchange losses and finance costs:** delayed receivables force borrowing, so higher rates hurt. Live rates: query_rates.
- **Price differential claims (PDCs):** when the government holds prices below cost, OMCs claim the difference. Delayed payment strains their cash.

## Sensitivities
- **Oil price spikes** give inventory gains but raise working-capital needs and hurt demand. The government may also cap or subsidise prices, which delays OMC recoveries.
- **PKR depreciation:** exchange losses on import payables.
- **Circular debt:** PSO's receivables (e.g. from SNGPL for RLNG and from power producers) drive its cash and dividend capacity.
- **Smuggled fuel** (e.g. from Iran) reduces formal sales. Enforcement drives volume recovery.
- **Petroleum levy and other levies** set in the budget raise pump prices and dampen demand.
- **Furnace oil** demand depends on power-plant dispatch. It jumps when gas/LNG is short and can fade quickly.

## What to watch
- Periodic fuel price notifications.
- Monthly OCAC sales data (search_news / read_news).
- OGRA margin notifications, PDC payments, and IMF talks on fuel subsidies.
- PSO quarterly results for receivables trends (query_announcements).

## Pitfalls
- Quarterly EPS swings mostly reflect inventory effects, not core earnings. Normalise them.
- A low P/E for PSO often reflects trapped receivables.
- Volume growth driven by one-off factors (e.g. furnace oil during gas shortages) may not last.
