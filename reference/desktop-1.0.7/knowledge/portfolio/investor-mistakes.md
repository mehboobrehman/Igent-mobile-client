# Common investor mistakes and how Igent helps avoid them

> General education, not personal financial advice.

Most losses come from behaviour, not from bad luck. Each mistake below has: why it hurts, a simple rule, and how Igent should respond.

**Igent's general tone:** calm and factual. Never shame the user. Compare with the safe rate after tax (savings/safe-rates). Offer a smaller size, a step-by-step entry, or a fund instead.

## FOMO and chasing rallies
- **Example:** a cement stock doubles in two months and everyone talks about it. You buy at the top.
- **Why it hurts:** the easy gain is gone and the risk of a sharp pullback is high.
- **Rule:** decide on value and thesis, not on recent gains. Buy in steps.
- **Igent:** show how far the price has run, what is already priced in, and suggest a small partial size or waiting.

## Panic selling in crashes
- **Example:** the KSE-100 falls sharply after political news. You sell everything at the low.
- **Why it hurts:** losses become permanent and you often miss the recovery.
- **Rule:** long-term money should not be in stocks if a fall would force you to sell. Re-check the thesis, not the price.
- **Igent:** ask whether the thesis has changed. Remind the user of their horizon. If money is needed soon, suggest moving that part to safe options calmly, not all at once.

## Herd behaviour and WhatsApp/Telegram tips
- **Example:** a "VIP group" posts a "sure-shot" penny stock target.
- **Why it hurts:** these are a classic pump-and-dump channel. Late buyers are the exit for early sellers.
- **Rule:** never act on unlicensed tips. Paid investment advice needs an SECP licence.
- **Igent:** check volume, free float and news. Flag manipulation risk. Link legal/kyc-aml-and-market-abuse and guides/complaints-and-safety.

## Loss aversion and holding losers
- **Example:** a stock is down 40% and the company's profits keep falling, but you wait to "get back to even".
- **Rule:** ask "would I buy this today at this price?" If no, the thesis is broken.
- **Igent:** separate thesis from price. Show where the money could work better.

## Selling winners too early
- **Example:** you sell a strong company after a 15% gain to "lock profit", then it compounds for years.
- **Rule:** trim when a position exceeds your cap or looks expensive, not just because it is up.
- **Igent:** suggest trimming part instead of selling all, if the thesis holds.

## Overconfidence and overtrading
- **Why it hurts:** brokerage, CDC and other charges, plus capital gains tax, are paid on every round trip. Some tax laws have taxed short holding periods more. See basics/tax and basics/orders.
- **Rule:** fewer, better decisions. Review on a schedule.
- **Igent:** show the cost and tax drag of frequent trades in rupees.

## Anchoring to your buy price
- **Example:** "I bought at Rs 200, I will sell only at Rs 200."
- **Rule:** the market does not know your buy price. Judge today's value.
- **Igent:** talk about current value and outlook, not the user's cost.

## Recency bias
- **Example:** after a strong year in stocks, assuming next year will be the same. Or after high debt fund returns, assuming they last when rates fall.
- **Rule:** look at full cycles, not the last year.
- **Igent:** mention that returns depend on the rate and market cycle.

## Confirmation bias
- **Rule:** before buying, write down what would prove you wrong.
- **Igent:** always list risks and the bear case on idea cards.

## Home bias and property obsession
- **Example:** all savings in plots, files or one house, because "property never falls".
- **Why it hurts:** property is illiquid, has high transaction costs and taxes, and can stay flat for years. File and plot scams are common.
- **Rule:** property can be part of wealth, not all of it.
- **Igent:** compare the realistic rental yield and costs with the safe rate. Suggest liquid options alongside.

## Leverage and margin trading
- **Why it hurts:** margin (MTS, margin financing) and futures amplify losses. A fall can trigger forced selling at the worst time. Financing costs add up. See basics/orders and basics/psx.
- **Rule:** never borrow to invest in stocks.
- **Igent:** decline to size leveraged ideas. Explain the downside in rupees.

## Ignoring tax and fees
- **Rule:** always compare after-tax, after-fee returns. Fund expense ratios and loads matter. See funds/amcs and basics/tax.
- **Igent:** show net figures, and remind non-filers of higher tax.

## Ignoring inflation (idle cash)
- **Example:** large sums in a current account or at home for years.
- **Why it hurts:** inflation quietly cuts buying power. See macro/inflation.
- **Igent:** suggest a money market fund or savings account for spare cash.

## Not diversifying
- **Example:** all money in one bank stock, or in five stocks from the same two sectors.
- **Rule:** spread across asset classes, sectors and names. See portfolio/risk-and-sizing.
- **Igent:** show sector and asset concentration from My Money.

## Chasing past fund returns
- **Why it hurts:** last year's top fund is often average next year. Debt fund returns mostly follow the policy rate.
- **Rule:** compare a fund with its own category and benchmark, over several years, after fees. See funds/fund-types.

## Mixing insurance and investment
- **Example:** buying an investment-linked life policy as your main savings plan.
- **Why it hurts:** charges can be high in early years and early surrender can lose money. The cover may be too small.
- **Rule:** buy protection as protection, and invest separately. Read the policy illustration and surrender terms.
- **Igent:** explain the trade-off. Insurance complaints go to the Federal Insurance Ombudsman (see legal/regulators-and-laws).

## Trusting guaranteed-return schemes
- **Example:** "fixed monthly profit" from a private person, company or app.
- **Why it hurts:** high guaranteed returns are a Ponzi red flag. Taking deposits without a licence is illegal.
- **Rule:** if it beats the safe rate by a lot with "no risk", walk away.
- **Igent:** warn clearly. Link legal/kyc-aml-and-market-abuse and guides/complaints-and-safety.
