# Building a portfolio: allocation, sizing and risk

> General education, not personal financial advice.

## First things first
Emergency fund and expensive debt come before any investing. See guides/start-here. Do not size an idea from money that is the emergency fund.

## Match money to time horizon
The date you need the money decides what it can go into. Risk comes second.

| Needed in | Fits | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Within 1 year | Bank savings, money market funds, short T-bills, short NSS | Stocks, equity funds, gold |
| 1 to 3 years | Money market, sovereign income or income funds, NSS certificates, term deposits | Large equity exposure |
| 5+ years | Equity funds, index funds/ETFs, single stocks, some gold, VPS for retirement | Keeping it all in cash |

Details: savings/bank-deposits, savings/national-savings, funds/fund-types, funds/pensions-vps. Between 3 and 5 years, blend: mostly income, some equity.

## Risk capacity vs risk tolerance
- **Risk capacity** is how much loss you can afford. It depends on income stability, dependants, horizon and other savings.
- **Risk tolerance** is how much loss you can stomach without panic selling.
- Use the **lower** of the two. A brave investor with unstable income still has low capacity.

## Core and satellite
- **Core (most of the money):** diversified funds and safe instruments. Money market and income funds, NSS, index or equity funds.
- **Satellite (a small part):** single stocks, sector bets, extra gold.
- A satellite mistake should hurt, not ruin. Beginners can skip the satellite entirely.

## Diversification
Spread across three levels:
1. **Asset classes:** cash and fixed income, equities, gold. They often do not fall together.
2. **Sectors:** the KSE-100 is free-float weighted and concentrated in a few large sectors (historically banks, oil and gas exploration, fertilizer, cement, power). Five "different" stocks from banks and E&P can be one bet. See basics/psx.
3. **Single names:** no one company should decide your outcome.

An index or equity fund gives sector and name diversification in one step.

## Position sizing rules for the agent
When an idea card suggests a size, Igent follows these rules:
- **Size by confidence.** Low confidence means a small size or "watch only".
- **Size by downside.** Ask "how much could this fall in a bad year?" A stock that can halve deserves less than a money market fund.
- **Smaller for illiquid or volatile names.** Thin volume, wide spreads, penny stocks and low free float all cut the size. See basics/orders.
- **Never above the user's cap.** The setting "Largest single idea (% of cash)" is a hard ceiling. Igent suggests less, never more.
- **Start partial, add later.** Suggest buying part now and the rest in steps, if the thesis still holds.
- **Keep a cash buffer.** Never suggest sizes that leave no spare cash for emergencies or later chances.
- **Count existing holdings.** If the user already owns the stock or sector, the new size includes what they hold.

### Expressing size as % of cash
- Size % = idea amount / investable cash x 100.
- Investable cash excludes the emergency fund and money needed within the idea's horizon.
- Example (made-up numbers): investable cash Rs 500,000, cap 10%. Maximum Rs 50,000. A medium-confidence, volatile stock might get 4% (Rs 20,000), starting with Rs 10,000.

| Confidence and risk | Suggested range (as share of the user's cap) |
|---|---|
| High confidence, liquid, diversified (fund) | Up to the full cap |
| Medium confidence, liquid large stock | About half the cap |
| Low confidence, or illiquid/volatile stock | A quarter of the cap or less, or watch only |

These ranges are Igent's house rules of thumb, not market standards.

## Rebalancing
Over time winners grow and the mix drifts.
- **Calendar:** check once or twice a year and move back to target.
- **Threshold:** rebalance only when an asset class drifts beyond a set band (for example 5 percentage points from target).
- Prefer adding new money to the underweight part instead of selling. Selling triggers costs and tax. See basics/tax.

## When to exit
- **Thesis broken vs price fell.** If the reason you bought has changed (earnings collapse, governance problem, policy shock), exit even at a loss. If only the price fell and the thesis holds, a fall alone is not a reason to sell.
- **Stop-loss.** Pros: caps losses and removes emotion. Cons: in volatile or thin PSX names it can trigger on noise, and fills can be far below the stop on a gap down. Use it as a review point, not a blind rule.
- **Taking profits.** Trim when a position grows beyond your cap or when the price now looks expensive against the thesis. Trimming part is often better than all or nothing.
- **Averaging down** only when the thesis still holds, the position stays within the cap, and you are not using emergency money.

## Liquidity risk
- **Thin shares:** some PSX stocks trade very little. You may not be able to sell at the screen price, or at all, in a panic. Locked limits can block selling for days.
- **Fund redemption timing:** requests after the cut-off get the next day's price. Payment then takes working days, faster for money market funds. See funds/amcs.
- **NSS and term deposits:** early encashment can cut or wipe out profit. See savings/safe-rates.

## Currency risk and real returns
- PKR assets lose value in dollar terms when the rupee weakens. Gold and E&P stocks have some dollar link. See macro/dollar and macro/gold.
- **Real return = return after tax minus inflation.** A positive nominal return can still lose buying power. See macro/inflation.
- Every risky idea must beat the safe rate after tax, plus a risk premium. See savings/safe-rates.

## Checklist before suggesting an idea card size
1. Is the emergency fund in place and excluded from investable cash?
2. Does the idea's horizon match when the user needs the money?
3. Does the expected after-tax return beat the matching safe rate by a clear margin?
4. How much could it fall in a bad year? Is the size small enough to survive that?
5. Is it liquid enough to exit? If not, cut the size.
6. What does the user already hold in this name, sector and asset class?
7. Is the size at or below the user's cap, and below it for low confidence?
8. Can it be bought in steps?
9. Is a fund a simpler way to get the same exposure?
10. Have the exit conditions (thesis-break signs) been written on the card?
