# Investor taxes for individuals

**Rates change every year.** Pakistan's tax rates are set by the Finance Act passed with each June budget (effective 1 July). This note explains the structure only. **Before quoting any rate, check the current FBR Withholding Tax Rate Card, NCCPL's CGT page, or recent budget news (search_news / read_news).** For big decisions, suggest a tax adviser.

"Filer" means a person on FBR's **Active Taxpayers List (ATL)**. Non-filers pay higher withholding rates on almost everything below, often about **double** the filer rate. See basics/filer-vs-non-filer.

## 1. Capital gains on listed shares (s.37A, collected by NCCPL)
- NCCPL computes and deducts CGT automatically when you sell through your broker.
- The rate depends on **filer status** and on **when the shares were bought**. Different purchase-date bands have had different rules. Some laws have used a flat rate regardless of holding period; older rules used lower rates for longer holding periods, and very old holdings were grandfathered.
- Gains and losses on securities in the same tax year are netted. Carry-forward of losses is limited; check current rules.
- Very high earners may also owe super tax. NCCPL collects it where it applies.

## 2. Dividends (s.150, deducted at source by the company or fund)
- Dividend tax is withheld before the cash reaches you. For individuals it is generally the final tax.
- Rates differ by type. Ordinary company dividends, certain power-project dividends, REIT dividends and mutual fund dividends can each have their own rate.
- For mutual funds, the dividend may be split by the fund's equity and debt income, with a higher rate on the debt-income part.

## 3. Mutual fund redemptions (CGT deducted by the AMC)
- The AMC deducts CGT when you redeem units.
- Gain = redemption price minus purchase cost (including any front load).
- The rate depends on filer status and on when the units were bought. Older units may follow older holding-period schedules.

## 4. Profit on debt (s.151)
- Withholding tax is deducted from profit on bank deposits, savings accounts, T-bills/PIBs held through banks, and National Savings schemes.
- Bank profit and National Savings profit have often had **different** rates. Compare them after tax.
- Some National Savings products for seniors, pensioners and families of martyrs (Behbood, Pensioners' Benefit, Shuhada) have had tax exemptions. Confirm on savings.gov.pk.
- Whether the withholding is final tax or adjustable can depend on amounts and law changes. Check current rules.

## 5. Property and gold (brief)
- Property: advance tax is charged on purchase (buyer) and sale (seller), with much higher rates for non-filers. Rates and other property taxes change in most budgets.
- Gold: gains on physical gold are taxed as capital gains on a capital asset under the normal rules. Jewellers charge sales tax on making/sale.

## 6. Dates
- The tax year runs 1 July to 30 June. The return deadline for individuals is usually 30 September but is often extended. See basics/filer-vs-non-filer.

## How Igent should use this
1. Find the user's filer status. Always state it in the answer.
2. Look up current rates (FBR rate card, NCCPL, recent budget news). Do not use remembered numbers.
3. Compare **post-tax** returns across options (bank profit, National Savings, fund dividends, fund redemption CGT, share dividends and CGT).

Example (made-up numbers): if bank profit is taxed at 20% and National Savings profit at 15%, then a 10% bank rate nets 8.0% and a 10% National Savings rate nets 8.5%. For a non-filer at double those rates, the nets would be 6.0% and 7.0%.
