# Filer vs non-filer

## What "filer" means
In Pakistan, a "filer" is a person whose name is on FBR's **Active Taxpayers List (ATL)**. Having an NTN alone is not enough.
- FBR has published the ATL once a year (on 1 March) and updated it weekly. Check fbr.gov.pk for the current schedule.
- You are on the ATL if you filed the income tax return for the latest tax year by the due date. Late filers can be added after paying a surcharge (s.182A). The surcharge amount is set by the Finance Act and changes.
- Check your status with FBR's "Active Taxpayer Status" online service. FBR has also offered an SMS check; confirm the current method on fbr.gov.pk.

## How to become a filer
1. Register on FBR's IRIS portal (or the Tax Asaan app) with your CNIC, mobile and email. Your CNIC becomes your NTN.
2. File the annual income tax return and wealth statement for the latest tax year. The usual deadline for individuals is 30 September, but FBR often extends it. Check FBR or recent news (search_news) for the current year's deadline.
3. If you file late, pay the ATL surcharge to be included. A separate late-filing penalty (s.182) may also apply.
4. List all investments (shares, funds, NSS, bank deposits, gold, property) in the wealth statement. The wealth statement must reconcile year to year.

## Why it matters for investing
Non-filers pay much higher withholding tax on most investment income. As a general pattern, the non-filer rate has often been about **double** the filer rate. This applies to:
- Dividends.
- Capital gains on listed shares and fund units.
- Bank profit.
- National Savings profit.
- Advance tax on property purchase and sale.

The exact rates change with each Finance Act (each June budget). Check FBR's current Withholding Tax Rate Card or recent news before quoting numbers. See basics/tax.

Non-filers also face restrictions on large economic transactions, such as buying property and vehicles and opening some accounts. These rules keep changing; check current FBR rules.

## Worked example
Example (made-up numbers): Rs 1,000,000 in a bank deposit earning 10% gross gives Rs 100,000 profit.
- Filer, if the tax rate is 20%: Rs 80,000 left (8.0% net).
- Non-filer, if the rate is double (40%): Rs 60,000 left (6.0% net).

The cost of filing (time, or an adviser's fee) is usually far smaller than the extra tax.

## Pitfalls
- Becoming a filer exposes your declared wealth to scrutiny. Investments must be explained by declared income. Advise honesty and proper documentation.
- Withholding is based on ATL status on the payment date. If you drop off the ATL, the higher rates apply immediately.
- Joint accounts: higher rates may apply if any holder is not on the ATL. Some items are apportioned between holders. Check current FBR rules.
