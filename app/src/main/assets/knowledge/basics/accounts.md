# Opening an account and costs

## Routes for a resident individual
1. **Broker account + CDC sub-account (most common).** Open a trading account with a PSX-licensed broker (TREC holder). The broker opens a CDC sub-account in your name under its participant account, and NCCPL registers you with a UIN. Shares sit in your sub-account. Cash sits with the broker in a segregated client account.
2. **CDC Investor Account (IAS).** Opened directly with CDC. You hold shares independently of the broker and move them to a broker when selling. Safer against broker default, but slower to trade.
3. **Mutual funds directly with an AMC.** No broker needed. Open online with the AMC (CNIC, bank account, KYC and the risk profiling questionnaire required by SECP suitability rules).
4. **Non-resident Pakistanis: Roshan Digital Account (RDA).** An SBP initiative launched in September 2020. It lets non-residents open accounts digitally with Pakistani banks and invest in PSX shares, mutual funds, government certificates and property. Mostly relevant to a resident user only for family members abroad.

Typical documents: CNIC, a bank account (IBAN) in your own name, proof of income source, filer status (NTN), a Zakat declaration (CZ-50) if seeking exemption, and a KYC/risk questionnaire. Most brokers offer online account opening and mobile apps.

Choosing a broker (generic, no endorsement): check it is licensed on PSX/SECP, its client-asset segregation record, app reliability, commission, and whether it offers CDC SMS/e-alerts. Always enable CDC/NCCPL SMS alerts so you see every movement in your holdings.

## Costs per trade (what to look for)
Exact amounts change and differ by broker. Always read the broker's current fee schedule.

| Item | How it works |
|---|---|
| Brokerage commission | Usually a % of trade value or a per-share amount, subject to a PSX-set minimum. Discount apps may charge less or a flat fee. |
| Sales tax on brokerage | Provincial services tax charged on the commission (rate depends on province). |
| CDC transaction fee | Small % of trade value per side. |
| PSX, SECP, NCCPL fees | Small regulatory fees per trade. |
| Other levies | Some brokers list further small charges on buys or sells; check the contract note. |
| CDC custody fee | Small periodic fee on holdings. |

Your **contract note** after each trade shows every charge. Add them up to get your real round-trip cost (buy plus sell) as a % of trade value. For a small retail account this is often around or above 1%, before capital gains tax. It matters most for short-term trading and small trades.

## Mutual fund costs
- Front-end load (often waived for online/direct investing), back-end load on some plans, and the annual Total Expense Ratio (TER) built into NAV. See funds/reading-nav.

## Tax plumbing
- NCCPL computes and deducts capital gains tax on listed shares. AMCs deduct CGT on fund redemptions. Companies deduct dividend tax at source. Filer (ATL) status is checked automatically. See basics/tax and basics/filer-vs-non-filer.

## Pitfalls
- Never give a broker blanket authority to trade. Never share CDC/IAS passwords.
- Small trades hit commission minimums, so very small trades are expensive in % terms.
- Idle cash with a broker earns little or nothing. Consider a money market fund.
