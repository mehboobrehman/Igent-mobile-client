# Banking in Pakistan: accounts and services

Profit rates, the SBP minimum profit rate and comparing deposits are in savings/bank-deposits. The account opening process is in guides/bank-deposits-step-by-step. This note covers account types and services.

Rules change. Confirm with the bank, sbp.org.pk, fbr.gov.pk or a qualified adviser before acting. This is not legal or tax advice.

## Who regulates banks
- The **State Bank of Pakistan (SBP)** licenses and supervises banks, microfinance banks, digital banks and payment services.
- The main banking law is the **Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962**.
- Before using any bank or app, check that it appears in SBP's lists of licensed institutions on sbp.org.pk.

## Kinds of banks
| Type | What it is |
|---|---|
| Commercial bank | Full-service bank with branches. Most also run Islamic banking branches. |
| Islamic bank | Works only under Shariah rules (see banking/islamic-banking). |
| Microfinance bank | Serves low-income people and small businesses. SBP-licensed. |
| Digital bank | SBP-licensed bank that works mainly through an app, with few or no branches. |
| Specialised bank | Government-owned banks for sectors such as agriculture or small business. |

## Account types
- **Current account**: for daily use. No profit.
- **Savings / PLS account**: earns profit on the balance. Fully liquid.
- **Term deposit (TDR)**: money locked for a fixed period. Breaking early usually costs profit.
- **Basic Banking Account (BBA)**: an SBP-mandated low-cost account for low-income people. No profit, no maintenance fee and a few free transactions each month.
- **Asaan Account**: an SBP account category for low-risk customers with simplified due diligence (fewer documents) but lower transaction limits. The **Asaan Mobile Account** is a related scheme for opening a basic account from a mobile phone.
- **Foreign currency account**: held in dollars or other currencies under SBP foreign exchange rules.
- **Roshan Digital Account**: for non-resident Pakistanis. See guides/overseas-pakistanis.
- **Joint account**: two or more holders. The mandate says who can operate it ("either or survivor", "jointly", etc.). Choose carefully.
- **Minor account**: opened for a child and operated by a parent or guardian.

## KYC and identity checks
- Banks must verify your identity under SBP's anti-money-laundering (AML/CFT) rules.
- Branches use **biometric verification** (fingerprint check against NADRA records). **NADRA Verisys** (an online check of CNIC details) is used where biometrics cannot be done.
- Typical documents: CNIC (or NICOP/passport), proof of income or source of funds, and a nominee. The full list is in guides/bank-deposits-step-by-step.
- Keep your CNIC valid. An expired CNIC can lead to account restrictions.

## How profit is calculated
- Banks use a method stated in the account terms. Common methods are the **daily product** (profit on each day's closing balance), the **monthly average balance**, or the **minimum balance** in the month.
- Profit is paid monthly, quarterly or half-yearly, depending on the product.
- Daily product is the fairest for you if your balance moves a lot. A minimum-balance method pays less if you withdraw mid-month.
- The SBP minimum profit rate rule is explained in savings/bank-deposits.

## Payments: IBAN and Raast
- **IBAN**: your account number in international format. Pakistani IBANs start with "PK" and are 24 characters. Use it for transfers, salary, dividends and fund redemptions.
- **Raast**: SBP's instant payment system, owned and operated by SBP. Payments reach the other account in seconds, around the clock.
- **Raast ID**: link your registered mobile number to your chosen IBAN through your bank's app, internet banking or a branch. Others can then pay you using just your mobile number.
- You can send or receive through Raast using an IBAN even without a Raast ID.
- SBP has stated that Raast person-to-person transfers carry no charges for customers. Transaction limits are set by each bank.

## Cards and digital banking safety
- Never share your PIN, OTP, password or full card details. Banks never ask for them.
- Turn on SMS alerts for every transaction.
- Use only the bank's official app, from the link on its official website.
- Report a lost card or suspicious transaction to the bank's helpline at once.
More scam warnings: guides/complaints-and-safety.

## Bank charges (SOBC)
- Each bank fixes its charges in advance for each half-year (January-June and July-December) in a **Schedule of Bank Charges (SOBC)**.
- The SOBC must be available at branches before the half-year starts. Charges cannot change during that half-year.
- Most banks also publish the SOBC on their website. Search the bank's site for "Schedule of Charges".

## Dormant accounts and unclaimed deposits
- **Dormant**: SBP treats an account as dormant if there has been no customer-initiated transaction or activity (such as a digital login) for one year.
- The bank must warn you before marking it dormant. Money can still come in, but withdrawals are blocked until you ask the bank to reactivate it.
- **Unclaimed**: deposits, cheques, drafts and bills with no activity for **ten years** must be surrendered to SBP under Section 31 of the Banking Companies Ordinance. Accounts of minors, governments and courts are excluded.
- SBP publishes year-wise lists of unclaimed deposits by bank on its website.
- **To claim**: apply at the branch where the account was opened (or the nearest branch of the same bank, or of the acquiring bank after a merger). Give a signed application and CNIC copy. Heirs need a succession certificate, with simpler documents allowed for small amounts. The bank sends the claim to SBP, which refunds through the bank.

## Deposit protection
- The **Deposit Protection Corporation (DPC)**, an SBP subsidiary set up under the Deposit Protection Corporation Act 2016, protects eligible depositors if SBP declares a member bank failed.
- Cover is up to a set amount **per depositor per bank**. All your accounts at one bank, conventional and Islamic, are added together. A joint balance is split between its holders.
- Check dpc.org.pk for the current protected amount and member banks.

## Zakat deduction
- Under the zakat law, banks deduct zakat at source from eligible deposits of Muslim account holders on the first day of Ramadan, if the balance that day is at or above the nisab notified for that year. Only that day's balance counts.
- **Exemption**: a Muslim who holds that their fiqh does not require deduction at source can file a **Zakat Declaration Form (CZ-50)** on oath. Banks usually want an original, attested or notarised form, some time before the deduction date. Ask your bank for its deadline.
- Non-Muslims are not subject to zakat. Tell the bank at account opening.
- A false declaration has legal consequences. For your personal obligation, ask a qualified scholar.

## Withholding taxes
- Banks deduct tax from profit. Rates often differ for filers (on the Active Taxpayers List) and non-filers. See basics/tax and basics/filer-vs-non-filer.
- Tax on cash withdrawals above a daily amount has applied to non-filers. Rules and rates change with each Finance Act. Check fbr.gov.pk.

## How to complain
1. **Your bank first**: complain in writing and keep the reference number. SBP rules give banks a fixed time limit for a final reply; ask the bank for it.
2. **Banking Mohtasib Pakistan** (bankingmohtasib.gov.pk): if the bank does not reply in time or you are unhappy with the reply. It does not cover microfinance banks.
3. **SBP's Sunwai portal and app** (sunwai.sbp.org.pk): lodge complaints against banks, microfinance banks and DFIs and track them. It routes cases to the bank, the Banking Mohtasib, or SBP itself for certain cases (for example microfinance banks).
4. A party unhappy with a Banking Mohtasib decision can appeal to the President within a set time.
See guides/complaints-and-safety for other regulators.
