# Textiles

Textiles have long been Pakistan's largest export industry, historically more than half of merchandise exports. On PSX the sector is fragmented. A few large composite and apparel exporters are liquid. Many small spinning mills are illiquid.

## Example listed companies (tickers)
- e.g. **ILP** (Interloop, hosiery/apparel for global brands), **NML** (Nishat Mills, composite; also holds large stakes in group companies), **GATM** (Gul Ahmed), **KTML** (Kohinoor Textile), **NCL** (Nishat Chunian), **FML** (Feroze1888).
- Plus many spinning and weaving mills.
- Check index and Shariah membership live per company.

## Export data
- **PBS** publishes monthly trade data, including the textile group (cotton yarn, cloth, knitwear, bedwear, readymade garments, towels).
- **APTMA** (All Pakistan Textile Mills Association) comments on exports and lobbies on energy and tax.
- Look at value-added categories (knitwear, garments) separately from low-value yarn and cloth. Igent finds the latest figures with search_news / read_news.

## Key drivers
- **Energy cost:** the biggest competitiveness issue. Industry compares Pakistan's power and gas tariffs with regional rivals. Policy on captive gas plants versus grid electricity is a recurring fight.
- **PKR/USD:** a weaker rupee raises rupee revenue for exporters, but imported cotton, polyester and machinery cost more. Live rate: query_rates.
- **Cotton crop:** domestic output and prices, plus import needs.
- **Export Facilitation Scheme (EFS):** duty- and tax-free imported inputs for exporters. Which inputs qualify changes by SRO. Spinners and value-added exporters lobby on opposite sides.
- **Interest rates and export finance:** working capital is heavy. Concessional export finance (SBP/EXIM Bank schemes) matters.
- **Global demand and trade access:** e.g. EU GSP+ status, US tariffs, and competition from Bangladesh, Vietnam and India.
- **Disruptions:** strikes, port problems and floods can delay shipments.

## What to watch
- Monthly PBS export data (textile group).
- Gas and electricity tariff notifications for industry, and RLNG availability.
- Budget and FBR changes on EFS, the export tax regime (final vs normal tax) and sales-tax refunds.
- PKR trend and global cotton prices.

## Pitfalls
- Many textile stocks are thinly traded. Check volume (query_prices) before recommending.
- Group holding values (e.g. NML's investments) can dominate valuation more than the textile business.
- Sales-tax refunds stuck with FBR can strain liquidity even when profits look fine.
