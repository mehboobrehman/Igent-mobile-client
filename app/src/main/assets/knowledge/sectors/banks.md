# Commercial Banks

Banks have historically been one of the largest sectors by weight in the KSE-100. They are also major dividend payers for income-focused investors. Check live index weights with query_index.

## Example listed banks (tickers)
- Large: e.g. **HBL**, **UBL**, **MCB**, **MEBL** (Meezan, Islamic), **NBP** (state-owned).
- Mid-size: e.g. **BAHL**, **BAFL**, **ABL**, **AKBL**, **FABL**, **BOP**, **BIPL**.
- Most conventional banks are not Shariah-compliant. Islamic banks (e.g. MEBL, BIPL) are. Shariah status and index membership change, so check them live.

## How banks earn money
- **Net interest income (NII)** is the main earnings line. It is the yield on loans and government securities minus the cost of deposits. Pakistani banks hold very large amounts of T-bills and PIBs, so NII follows the policy rate and how quickly assets reprice compared with deposits.
- **Non-interest income** comes from fees, FX income and capital gains on securities. It can swing a lot from quarter to quarter.
- **Provisions** are charges for bad loans. They rise in downturns and after rate shocks.
- SBP publishes sector data (deposits, advances, NPLs, profitability). Company results come through query_announcements.

## Rate sensitivity (rules of thumb)
- **Rate cuts:** NII margins usually compress over time because assets reprice down faster than deposits. Bond portfolios can book capital gains in the short term.
- **Rate hikes** work the other way, with a lag. Deposit costs catch up later.
- **Minimum deposit rate (MDR) rule:** SBP requires a minimum profit rate on certain savings deposits, linked to the SBP rate corridor. SBP changes the rule's scope and thresholds from time to time. A narrower rule gives banks room to cut deposit costs, which supports margins. Check the latest SBP circular.
- Islamic banks have historically had a cheap current-account deposit base, which gave them higher spreads.
- Live policy rate, KIBOR and T-bill yields: query_rates.

## Tax (important for valuation)
- Banks have usually paid a higher standard corporate tax rate than other companies, plus super tax on top. Rates are set in each year's Finance Act and change often.
- Governments have also used bank-specific measures, such as a past tax linked to the advances-to-deposit ratio (later abolished).
- Check the latest Finance Act before comparing after-tax earnings across years.

## What to watch
- SBP MPC decisions (meeting dates on sbp.org.pk).
- Quarterly results and interim dividends.
- Asset quality and provisions, government borrowing needs, and new bank taxes in budgets.

## Pitfalls
- Do not judge banks on dividend yield alone. A high payout can be cut when NII falls or capital ratios tighten.
- One-off securities gains can inflate a quarter's profit.
- State ownership (e.g. NBP) brings policy and pension-liability risks.
