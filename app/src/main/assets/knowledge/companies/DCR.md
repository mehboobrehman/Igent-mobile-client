**Symbol:** DCR | **Sector:** Real Estate Investment Trust

Dolmen City REIT is a listed, closed-end, perpetual, rental REIT scheme. It was set up under a trust deed dated 20 January 2015 and launched in 2015. Its manager describes it as Pakistan's first REIT scheme.

**Who runs it**
- **REIT Management Company (RMC):** Arif Habib Dolmen REIT Management Limited (AHDRML), a joint venture of the Arif Habib Group and the Dolmen Group.
- **Trustee:** Central Depository Company of Pakistan Limited (CDC).

**Properties (both in Clifton, Karachi, part of the Dolmen City project on the seafront)**
- **Dolmen Mall Clifton:** a large three-level shopping mall with local and international brands, anchor stores and a food court.
- **The Harbour Front:** an office tower let to local and multinational companies.

**Income sources**
- Rent from mall tenants (retail, food and anchors).
- Rent from office tenants at The Harbour Front.
- Any rise in property value adds to NAV, but that is a revaluation gain, not cash.

Rental REITs pay out most of their income, so DCR is held mainly for its distributions. Compare the yield on market price (not on face value) with the after-tax safe rate (savings/safe-rates). REIT dividend tax can differ from ordinary dividends (see basics/tax).

**What to watch each quarter**
- Occupancy of the mall and of the office tower.
- Rental income growth and lease renewals.
- Operating costs and net income.
- Distributions declared (query_announcements).
- NAV per unit after each valuation, and the split between rental income and revaluation gains.
- Market price vs NAV: the discount, and whether it is wider or narrower than usual.

**Key risks**
- **Concentration:** only two properties, in one city and one neighbourhood.
- **Retail footfall:** weak consumer spending, inflation or new rival malls can hurt mall tenants and rents.
- **Office demand:** a large tenant leaving the tower would cut income.
- **Security and law-and-order events in Karachi** can close the mall or cut footfall.
- **Interest rates:** when the policy rate rises, safe rates rise and the unit price tends to fall (macro/policy-rate).
- **Liquidity:** trading can be thin and spreads wide. Use limit orders.
