# Start here: the order to do things

> Igent gives general education, not personalised financial advice. Check current rules and rates before acting.

Most beginner losses come from doing things in the wrong order. Follow these steps from top to bottom.

## Step 1: Build an emergency fund
1. Work out your essential monthly spending (rent, food, bills, school fees, loan instalments).
2. Keep several months of that amount somewhere safe and quick to reach.
3. Good homes for it: a bank savings account, a money market fund, or a short-term National Savings product you can encash. See savings/bank-deposits, funds/fund-types and savings/national-savings.
4. Do not put emergency money in stocks, gold or long-lock certificates.
5. People with unstable income, dependants or one earner in the family should keep a bigger buffer.

## Step 2: Pay off expensive debt first
- Credit card debt, personal loans and informal loans often cost more than any investment can reliably earn.
- Paying off a loan is a guaranteed "return" equal to its interest or markup rate.
- Never borrow to invest in stocks. That includes margin (MTS) and futures. See basics/psx.

## Step 3: Know your goal and horizon
Write down for each goal:
- What it is (house deposit, child's education, Hajj, retirement, wedding).
- When you need the money (the horizon).
- How much loss you could bear without selling in panic.

Rule of thumb:
| Horizon | Suitable options |
|---|---|
| Under 1 year | Bank savings, money market fund, short-term NSS |
| 1 to 3 years | Money market, sovereign or income funds, NSS certificates, term deposits |
| 3 to 5 years | Add some asset-allocation or balanced funds |
| 5+ years | Equity funds, index funds/ETFs, then individual stocks |

## Step 4: Know the safe rate
- The "safe rate" is what you can earn with almost no risk (T-bills, money market funds, NSS).
- Any risky idea must beat the safe rate **after tax**, plus a margin for risk.
- See savings/safe-rates. Ask Igent "what is the safe rate now?" to get live figures.

## Step 5: Become a filer
- Non-filers pay much higher tax on profit, dividends and capital gains.
- Filing also lets you show where your money came from.
- See guides/become-a-filer and basics/filer-vs-non-filer.

## Step 6: Pick a route by goal and risk
Climb this ladder one rung at a time. Move up only when you understand the current rung.
1. **Savings:** bank deposits and National Savings. Capital is safe; returns may lag inflation. See guides/bank-deposits-step-by-step and guides/national-savings-step-by-step.
2. **Money market funds:** cash parking with daily liquidity. See guides/mutual-funds-step-by-step.
3. **Income and asset-allocation funds:** a bit more return and a bit more risk.
4. **Equity funds and index funds/ETFs:** stock market exposure with diversification done for you. See funds/etfs.
5. **Individual stocks:** only with money you will not need for 5+ years, and after learning the basics. See guides/stocks-step-by-step.

Gold can be a small slice for diversification at any rung. See guides/gold-step-by-step.

## Step 7: Shariah-compliant option
- Almost every rung has an Islamic version: Islamic bank deposits, Shariah-compliant NSS products (check savings.gov.pk), Islamic money market, income and equity funds, Islamic ETFs, and stocks on the PSX-KMI list.
- See shariah/screening and shariah/kmi-30.
- Igent is not a Shariah authority. Follow your own scholar on detailed questions.

## Step 8: Start small and invest regularly
- Begin with an amount you can afford to lose part of while you learn.
- Invest a fixed amount every month (a "systematic investment plan", SIP). Many AMCs let you set up a monthly auto-debit.
- Regular investing buys more units when prices are low and fewer when high. It removes the urge to time the market.
- Review once or twice a year, not every hour.

Example (made-up numbers): Rs 10,000 a month into an equity fund. Month 1 NAV Rs 100 buys 100 units. Month 2 NAV Rs 80 buys 125 units. Average cost is Rs 88.9 per unit, below the simple average price of Rs 90.

## Common beginner mistakes
- **Tips from social media:** WhatsApp, Telegram, YouTube and TikTok "sure-shot" calls are a classic pump-and-dump channel. Unlicensed advice is illegal.
- **Leverage:** margin financing and futures can wipe out your capital quickly.
- **Chasing hot stocks:** buying after a stock has already doubled, often a penny stock with high volume.
- **No diversification:** putting everything in one stock or one sector.
- **Panic selling:** selling everything after a fall and missing the recovery.
- **Ignoring costs and tax:** frequent trading eats returns. See basics/accounts and basics/tax.
- **Guaranteed-return schemes:** anyone promising fixed high monthly profit is a red flag. See guides/complaints-and-safety.
- **Investing money needed soon:** money for next year's fees does not belong in stocks.

## Quick checklist before your first investment
- [ ] Emergency fund in place
- [ ] Expensive debt paid off or on a clear plan
- [ ] Goal and horizon written down
- [ ] Filer status checked
- [ ] Safe rate known
- [ ] Route chosen from the ladder
- [ ] Only licensed brokers, AMCs and banks used
