# From zero to your first share

Before starting, read guides/start-here. Stocks suit money you will not need for 5+ years. Background: basics/psx, basics/accounts, basics/orders.

## Step 1: Choose a licensed broker
Only use a broker licensed by SECP and a PSX TREC holder.

How to verify:
1. Check the broker's name on the PSX website's list of brokers/TREC holders.
2. Check SECP's list of licensed securities brokers (secp.gov.pk).
3. Make sure the app or website you use belongs to that broker (same name, official domain, linked from the broker's site or PSX).
4. Search recent news for complaints or SECP/PSX penalties against the broker.

What to compare (no broker is recommended here):
- Commission and other charges (ask for the full fee schedule).
- App and website quality, and whether it works when markets are busy.
- Online account opening and customer support.
- Research quality, if you want it.
- Whether a CDC Investor Account (IAS) option is supported for extra safety (see basics/accounts).
- Any fee for inactivity, custody or account maintenance.

## Step 2: Prepare documents
Typical list (the broker will tell you the exact one):
- Valid CNIC (NICOP for overseas Pakistanis; see guides/overseas-pakistanis).
- A bank account in your own name (IBAN). Payments must come from and go to this account.
- Proof of income or source of funds (salary slip, business proof, pension, etc.).
- NTN / filer status (see guides/become-a-filer).
- Zakat exemption declaration (CZ-50) only if you want to claim exemption.
- Nominee details, if offered.
- Mobile number and email registered in your own name.

## Step 3: Open the account online
1. Fill the broker's online form, or visit a branch.
2. Complete KYC: biometric or video verification, selfie or document upload.
3. Answer the risk profile and suitability questions honestly.
4. Read the account opening agreement, especially the parts on fees, margin and trading authority.
5. Do not sign any blanket authority letting the broker trade for you.
6. Wait for approval. The broker will send login details.

## Step 4: CDC sub-account and NCCPL UIN
- The broker opens a **CDC sub-account** in your name. Your shares are held there electronically.
- **NCCPL** registers you with a **UIN** (Unique Identification Number), usually your CNIC.
- You should receive confirmation from CDC and NCCPL by SMS or email. If you do not, ask the broker.

## Step 5: Turn on CDC alerts and CDC Access
1. Make sure your own mobile number and email are registered with CDC (not the broker's or an agent's).
2. Enable CDC SMS and e-alerts. Every movement in your shares will be reported to you.
3. Register for CDC's online access service (CDC Access) to see holdings and statements yourself. Check cdcpakistan.com for current registration steps.
4. Keep your CDC and broker passwords private. No broker staff need them.

## Step 6: Fund the account
1. Get the broker's **client account** details from its official website or app.
2. Transfer from your own bank account (IBFT, online banking or cheque).
3. **Never** pay into a personal account of any broker employee or agent. Never hand over cash.
4. Keep the transfer receipt. Confirm the money appears in your trading account.

## Step 7: Place your first order
1. Pick a stock you have researched. Start with large, liquid companies (KSE-30 or KSE-100 members).
2. Check the price, best bid/ask, spread and today's traded volume/value. Ask Igent for live prices (query_prices).
3. Choose **Buy**, enter the symbol and the quantity. The regular market allows small quantities (check the lot size shown for the symbol; see basics/psx).
4. Use a **limit order** at or near the current ask. Avoid market orders in thin stocks.
5. Check the order summary, including estimated charges, then confirm.
6. Look at the order status: pending, partly filled, filled or cancelled.
7. Avoid pre-open orders until you understand them. They cannot be cancelled during matching.

Example (made-up numbers): a stock shows bid Rs 99.90 and ask Rs 100.00. A limit buy of 50 shares at Rs 100.00 costs about Rs 5,000 plus charges. Your contract note shows the exact charges.

## Step 8: Settlement and confirmation
- Trades settle on the PSX settlement cycle (see basics/psx for the current cycle).
- You receive a **contract note** from the broker. Check price, quantity and every charge.
- After settlement, the shares appear in your CDC sub-account. You should get a CDC SMS.

## Step 9: Check your holdings
- Compare the broker app with your CDC statement or CDC Access every month.
- If shares are missing or there are trades you did not place, complain at once (see guides/complaints-and-safety).

## Step 10: Dividends
- Companies announce dividends with results. To get one, you must own the shares before the book closure / entitlement date.
- The cash is paid by the company **directly to your registered bank account (IBAN)**, not through the broker.
- Tax is deducted at source before payment. Zakat may also be deducted unless you filed an exemption. See basics/tax.
- Keep your IBAN updated with CDC through your broker so payments do not get stuck.
- The share price usually drops by about the dividend on the ex-date.

## Step 11: Bonus and right shares (basics)
- **Bonus shares:** free extra shares credited to your CDC account. The price adjusts down, so your total value does not jump.
- **Right shares:** an offer to buy new shares, usually at a discount, in proportion to your holding. You can subscribe, sell the right (it trades for a short time), or let it lapse. If you ignore it, your stake is diluted. Watch for announcements (query_announcements).

## Step 12: Sell and withdraw cash
1. Place a **limit sell** order.
2. After settlement, the money appears as cash with the broker.
3. Request a withdrawal in the app. The broker pays it to your own registered bank account.
4. NCCPL deducts capital gains tax automatically on gains. See basics/tax.
5. Do not leave large idle cash with a broker. It usually earns little.

## Step 13: Keep records for tax
Keep in one folder:
- Contract notes and monthly broker statements.
- CDC statements.
- Dividend tax certificates or payment advice from companies.
- The annual capital gains tax statement/certificate from NCCPL. You can usually get it through your broker or NCCPL's investor services; check nccpl.com.pk.
- Use these when filing your return and wealth statement (guides/become-a-filer).

## Red flags of fraud
- Someone offers to trade "on your behalf" for a share of profit.
- You are asked to pay into a personal bank account or a mobile wallet.
- A broker or app is not on the PSX/SECP lists.
- Guaranteed returns or "insider" tips.
- You stop receiving CDC SMS alerts, or your registered mobile was changed without your request.
- Pressure to buy margin products or unknown penny stocks.

See guides/complaints-and-safety for where to complain.
