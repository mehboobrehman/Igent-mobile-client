# Owning gold safely

Gold can protect against rupee weakness and inflation, but it pays no income. Treat it as a modest slice of a portfolio, not the whole plan. Price mechanics, units and tax are in macro/gold.

Ask Igent for today's gold rate and dollar rate (query_rates).

## Step 1: Choose how to own gold
| Way | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Bars and coins (24K) | Investment, smaller spread | Purity checks, storage, theft |
| Jewellery (often 22K or lower) | Wearing and family use | Making charges, buy-back deductions |
| Gold funds (AMC commodity funds), where available | No storage, small amounts, sell at NAV | Loads, expense ratio |
| PMEX gold contracts via a PMEX-registered broker | Experienced traders | Leverage and margin calls; not for most savers |

- For gold funds, follow guides/mutual-funds-step-by-step. Ask Igent (query_navs) which gold funds currently exist.
- Check funds/etfs before assuming any gold ETF is listed on PSX.
- For PMEX, use only brokers registered with PMEX (regulated by SECP). Check pmex.com.pk. Understand margin before trading anything with leverage.

## Step 2: Learn the units and purity
- 1 tola = 11.6638 grams. Rates are quoted per tola and per 10 grams.
- 24 karat is the purest commercial gold. 22K is 22/24 pure. 21K and 18K are less pure.
- Jewellery is rarely 24K. Make sure the rate you are quoted matches the karat.

Example (made-up numbers): if 24K is Rs 240,000 per tola, the gold in 22K is worth about 240,000 x 22/24 = Rs 220,000 per tola, before making charges.

## Step 3: Check the fair price before you go
1. Get today's sarafa (APGJSA) rate from Igent or the news.
2. Compare it with the international price converted to rupees (formula in macro/gold).
3. Ask two or three dealers for both their buy and their sell quotes.
4. The gap between a dealer's buy and sell price is your hidden cost.

## Step 4: Buy physical gold safely
- [ ] Buy from an established jeweller or dealer with a fixed shop and a good reputation.
- [ ] Get the karat in writing. Ask whether the piece carries a karat stamp or a testing-lab certificate.
- [ ] Have it tested if unsure. Many markets have independent karat-testing shops.
- [ ] Watch the piece being weighed on a calibrated scale. Note the weight in grams and tola.
- [ ] Ask for making charges (jewellery) separately from the gold value.
- [ ] Ask for the **buy-back policy**: the deduction when you sell back, and whether it differs for gold bought elsewhere.
- [ ] Get a **printed receipt** with shop name, date, item, karat, weight, rate, making charges, taxes and total.
- [ ] Pay by bank transfer or card where possible, so there is a record. Avoid carrying large cash.
- [ ] For jewellery with stones, ask whether stone weight is included. Stones are not gold.

## Step 5: Store and insure
- Home storage carries theft risk. A bank locker is safer, but banks generally do not insure locker contents. Read the locker agreement.
- Some insurers offer home contents or jewellery cover. Ask about gold specifically.
- Keep receipts, photos and weights safe, and record holdings in Igent's My Money.
- Do not tell many people what you keep at home.

## Step 6: Sell
1. Check today's rate (Igent or the news).
2. Take your receipt to the original dealer first. Many give better terms on their own items.
3. Get quotes from two or three dealers. Ask each which karat they will test for and what they deduct.
4. Watch the testing and weighing.
5. Prefer payment by bank transfer or cheque for a record.
6. For gold funds, redeem through the AMC at NAV (see guides/mutual-funds-step-by-step).

Example (made-up numbers): you bought a 1 tola 22K chain for Rs 230,000, including Rs 10,000 making charges. A year later its gold value is Rs 240,000, but the dealer deducts 5% on buy-back. You receive Rs 228,000. Gold rose, yet you lost Rs 2,000 because of making charges and the deduction.

## Step 7: How the local price moves
- Local price is roughly the international price x dollar rate x conversion, plus a local premium or discount. See macro/gold.
- Rupee depreciation lifts local gold even if world gold is flat.
- Local premiums can widen in crises and shrink later.
- Gold can fall for long periods. Do not buy only because it "always goes up".

## Step 8: Tax and wealth statement
- Jewellers charge sales tax. Rates change in budgets.
- Gains on selling gold can be taxable as capital gains. Rules change; see basics/tax and macro/gold.
- Filers must declare gold (weight and value) in the wealth statement. Keep purchase receipts to prove cost and source. Inherited or gifted gold should also be documented. See guides/become-a-filer.

## Red flags
- "Gold committees" or schemes promising fixed monthly returns on gold.
- Prices far below the sarafa rate (often impure or stolen gold).
- No receipt, or a receipt without karat and weight.
- Online sellers with no shop address who want advance payment.
- Leveraged "gold trading" apps that are not PMEX-registered brokers.
