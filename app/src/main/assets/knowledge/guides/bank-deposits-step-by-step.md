# Bank savings and term deposits

Bank deposits are the simplest place for emergency money and short-term savings. Product rules, the SBP minimum profit rate and deposit protection are explained in savings/bank-deposits. This guide covers the process.

## Step 1: Decide what you need
| Need | Product |
|---|---|
| Daily spending | Current account (no profit) |
| Emergency fund, money you may need any time | Savings / PLS account |
| Money you will not touch for a fixed period | Term deposit (TDR) or deposit certificate |
| Shariah-compliant | Islamic bank or Islamic window savings / term account |

## Step 2: Conventional vs Islamic
- **Conventional banks** pay a stated profit (interest) rate. SBP sets a minimum profit rate on many PKR savings deposits (see savings/bank-deposits).
- **Islamic banks and windows** use profit-sharing (mudarabah) pools. They announce an *expected* rate, which can change monthly. They are supervised by SBP and a Shariah board.
- Both are covered by the same deposit protection system.
- Compare Islamic products with Islamic peers and with conventional options after tax.

## Step 3: Compare banks
1. Collect current profit rates from banks' official websites or branches. Ask Igent for the policy rate and the safe rate (query_rates, savings/safe-rates).
2. Check how profit is calculated: monthly average balance or minimum balance.
3. Check how often profit is paid: monthly, quarterly or half-yearly.
4. Look for conditions behind "up to" rates (minimum balance, tiered rates, number of withdrawals).
5. Check account fees, debit card charges and charges for falling below a minimum balance.
6. For term deposits, compare tenor, payout frequency and **early-break rules**.
7. Consider the bank's strength (credit rating published by rating agencies) and service quality.
No bank is recommended here. Pick by these criteria.

## Step 4: Open the account
Documents (typical):
- Original CNIC (NICOP or passport for overseas Pakistanis; see guides/overseas-pakistanis).
- Proof of income or source of funds (salary slip, business documents, pension).
- Proof of address if your CNIC address is out of date.
- NTN / filer status (affects tax on profit).
- Zakat exemption declaration (CZ-50), only if claiming exemption.
- Nominee or next-of-kin details.

Steps:
1. Visit a branch, or use the bank's official app or website for digital account opening.
2. Complete the form and biometric verification.
3. Answer KYC questions about income and expected transactions honestly.
4. Choose the account type and profit payout option.
5. Register for SMS alerts and internet/mobile banking.
6. Receive your account number, IBAN, cheque book and debit card as applicable.

## Step 5: Open a term deposit
1. Choose the amount and tenor.
2. Choose profit payout: monthly, at maturity, or another option offered.
3. Choose what happens at maturity: auto-renew at the then-current rate, or credit to your savings account.
4. **Ask for the early-break rules in writing.** Typically, breaking early pays a lower rate, or a reduced profit, or charges a penalty. Some banks pay no profit if broken very early.
5. Keep the deposit receipt or certificate and note the maturity date.

Example (made-up numbers): Rs 500,000 in a 1-year term deposit at 10%. Held to maturity, profit is Rs 50,000 before tax. If broken after 6 months and the bank pays the 6-month rate of 8% for that period, profit is about Rs 20,000 instead of Rs 25,000.

## Step 6: Deposit protection
- The Deposit Protection Corporation (DPC), an SBP subsidiary, protects deposits up to a set limit per depositor per bank, if SBP declares a member bank failed.
- Check dpc.org.pk for the current limit and coverage rules.
- If your savings are far above the limit, consider spreading them across banks or into government-backed options (guides/national-savings-step-by-step).

## Step 7: Digital banks and branchless options
- SBP has licensed digital banks that operate mainly through apps. They are SBP-regulated banks.
- Branchless banking wallets also offer basic accounts.
- Before using any digital bank or wallet, confirm it appears in SBP's list of licensed institutions on sbp.org.pk.
- Check whether its deposits are covered by DPC (dpc.org.pk) and what limits apply to the account level.

## Step 8: Tax
- The bank deducts withholding tax from profit. Non-filers pay a higher rate. See basics/tax.
- Zakat may be deducted from savings accounts unless you filed an exemption.
- Ask the bank for an annual tax deduction certificate and an account statement for your return and wealth statement (guides/become-a-filer).

## Checklist
- [ ] Product matches need (savings vs term)
- [ ] Rates and calculation basis compared across banks
- [ ] Early-break rules in writing
- [ ] SMS alerts on
- [ ] Nominee given
- [ ] Filer status registered with the bank

## Red flags
- Calls or messages asking for your PIN, OTP, card number or password. Banks never ask for these.
- A "bank officer" offering special high rates through a personal account.
- Links in SMS or WhatsApp to "update your account". Use only the official app or website.
