# Becoming a filer

A "filer" is a person on FBR's **Active Taxpayers List (ATL)**. Filers pay lower withholding tax on profit, dividends and capital gains than non-filers. Why it matters and the tax mechanics: basics/filer-vs-non-filer and basics/tax.

This guide is general. For complex income (business, property, foreign income), use a tax adviser.

## What you need before starting
- CNIC.
- A mobile number registered in your own name (FBR sends codes to it).
- An email address you control.
- Your income details for the tax year (1 July to 30 June): salary certificate, business records, pension, rent, etc.
- Tax already deducted: certificates from your employer, bank, broker/NCCPL, AMC, CDNS, and receipts for tax on phone bills, vehicle tokens or electricity where relevant.
- A list of everything you own and owe on 30 June (for the wealth statement).

## Step 1: Register on IRIS
1. Go to FBR's IRIS portal (reach it from fbr.gov.pk). FBR has also offered a mobile app (Tax Asaan) for registration and simple returns; check fbr.gov.pk for current apps.
2. Choose registration for an individual not yet registered.
3. Enter your CNIC, mobile number and email.
4. Enter the verification codes sent to your phone and email.
5. Receive your login details. For individuals, your CNIC number serves as your NTN.
6. Log in and complete your profile (address, income sources, bank account).

Getting an NTN alone does **not** make you a filer. You must file a return.

## Step 2: File the income tax return
1. Log in to IRIS and open the return form for the latest tax year (choose the form for your type of income, e.g. salaried individual).
2. Enter income: salary, business, property rent, capital gains, profit on deposits, dividends.
3. Enter tax already deducted. Much of it may appear automatically from withholding data, but check every item against your certificates.
4. Enter deductible allowances and tax credits you are eligible for (for example VPS pension contributions, within legal limits; see guides/mutual-funds-step-by-step).
5. Check the computed tax. If tax is payable, generate a payment slip (PSID) and pay through your bank's app or branch. Enter the payment details in the return.
6. Submit the return.

## Step 3: File the wealth statement
Individuals filing a return generally also file a wealth statement in IRIS.
1. List assets on 30 June: bank balances, NSS, mutual funds, shares, gold (weight and value), property, vehicles, cash, loans given.
2. List liabilities: loans taken, credit card balances.
3. Complete the **wealth reconciliation**: opening wealth + income + gifts/inheritance - expenses = closing wealth.
4. Keep supporting documents: bank statements, CDC and NCCPL statements, AMC statements, NSS certificates, gold receipts, property papers.

Example (made-up numbers): opening wealth Rs 2,000,000. Income after tax Rs 1,200,000. Household expenses Rs 900,000. Closing wealth should be Rs 2,300,000. If your listed assets add up to more, you must explain the gap (for example a gift, with documents).

## Step 4: Get on the Active Taxpayers List
- If you file by the due date, you are usually included in the ATL when FBR updates it.
- If you file late, you may need to pay an ATL surcharge to be included, and a late-filing penalty may apply. Amounts are set by law and change. Check fbr.gov.pk.
- FBR publishes the ATL each year and updates it regularly. Check fbr.gov.pk for the current schedule.

## Step 5: Check your ATL status
1. Use FBR's online "Active Taxpayer Status" check on fbr.gov.pk with your CNIC.
2. FBR has also offered an SMS check. Confirm the current method on fbr.gov.pk.
3. Once active, tell your bank, broker, AMC and CDNS so they apply filer rates. Most check the ATL automatically, but confirm.
4. Tell Igent your filer status so after-tax comparisons are correct.

## Step 6: Deadlines (concept)
- The tax year runs 1 July to 30 June.
- The usual return deadline for individuals is **30 September**, but FBR often extends it.
- Check fbr.gov.pk or recent news (ask Igent to search the news) for this year's deadline.
- You must file every year to stay on the ATL. Missing a year can drop you off and bring back non-filer rates.

## Why it matters for investors
- Lower tax on bank and NSS profit, dividends, and capital gains on shares and funds.
- Lower advance tax on property and vehicle transactions.
- A documented record of your wealth, which helps prove the source of funds for future investments.
See basics/filer-vs-non-filer for a worked comparison.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking an NTN alone makes you a filer.
- Leaving investments out of the wealth statement.
- Not reconciling wealth year to year.
- Ignoring tax deduction certificates and paying tax twice.
- Letting an unknown "agent" use your IRIS password. Use a qualified tax adviser and keep control of your login.
