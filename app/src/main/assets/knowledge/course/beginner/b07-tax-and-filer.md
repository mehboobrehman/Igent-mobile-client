## The Big Idea

Tax quietly takes a slice of almost every investment return in Pakistan, usually before the money even reaches you. How big that slice is depends a lot on one thing: whether you are a **filer**. Becoming a filer is one of the easiest ways to keep more of your own returns.

## Explain It Like I'm New

### What is a filer?

In Pakistan, a **filer** is a person whose name is on FBR's **Active Taxpayers List (ATL)**. **FBR** is the Federal Board of Revenue, the government body that collects taxes.

You get on the ATL by **filing your income tax return** for the latest tax year. Just registering and getting an **NTN** (National Tax Number) is not enough. For individuals, your CNIC number serves as your NTN, but you still have to file the return.

A **non-filer** is anyone not on the list. Non-filers usually pay much higher tax on investment income. As a general pattern, the non-filer rate has often been about **double** the filer rate.

### Withholding tax: tax taken at the source

**Withholding tax** means tax is cut before you receive the money. You do not have to pay it separately; the payer deducts it and sends it to FBR.

| Income | Who deducts the tax |
|---|---|
| Profit on bank deposits | Your bank |
| Profit on National Savings | CDNS |
| Profit on T-bills held through a bank | The bank |
| Dividends from shares | The company paying the dividend |
| Dividends from mutual funds | The fund |

A **dividend** is a share of a company's or fund's profit paid out to its owners. Each type of income can have its own tax rate, and filers pay less than non-filers.

### Capital gains tax: tax on selling at a profit

A **capital gain** is the profit you make when you sell something for more than you paid. If you buy fund units for Rs 100,000 and later sell them for Rs 120,000, your capital gain is Rs 20,000.

**Capital gains tax (CGT)** is the tax on that profit.
- On shares listed on PSX, **NCCPL** (the National Clearing Company of Pakistan) works out and deducts CGT automatically when you sell through your broker.
- On mutual funds, the **AMC** deducts it when you redeem your units.
- The rate depends on filer status and can depend on when you bought. The rules have changed many times.

You pay CGT on the **gain**, not on the whole amount you sell.

### Rates change every year

Tax rates are set by the **Finance Act** passed with each June budget, effective from 1 July. That is why this course never gives you a rate as a fact. To get today's rates, check FBR's **Withholding Tax Rate Card** on fbr.gov.pk, or ask Igent in Chat, which can look up recent news. For big decisions, a qualified tax adviser helps.

### How to become a filer

1. **Register on IRIS**, FBR's online portal (reach it from fbr.gov.pk), with your CNIC, a mobile number in your own name and your email.
2. **File your income tax return** for the latest tax year (1 July to 30 June). Enter your income and the tax already deducted from it.
3. **File your wealth statement**: a list of what you own and owe on 30 June, including bank balances, funds, shares, National Savings and gold. It must reconcile with your income and spending.
4. **File on time.** The usual deadline for individuals is 30 September, though FBR often extends it. Late filers may have to pay a surcharge to be added to the ATL.
5. **Check your status** with FBR's online Active Taxpayer Status service.
6. **Repeat every year.** Miss a year and you can drop off the list.

Keep control of your own IRIS password. If you use a tax adviser, choose a qualified one.

### Being honest matters

Your wealth statement shows what you own, so your investments should be explained by your declared income. That is a good thing: it gives you a clean record of where your money came from.

## A Pakistani Example

Ayesha and Bilal each have **Rs 1,000,000** in a bank deposit earning 10% a year, which is Rs 100,000 of profit. Using made-up example tax rates:

| | Ayesha (filer) | Bilal (non-filer) |
|---|---|---|
| Example tax rate | 20% | 40% (double) |
| Tax deducted | Rs 20,000 | Rs 40,000 |
| Profit kept | **Rs 80,000** | **Rs 60,000** |
| Net return | 8.0% | 6.0% |

Bilal loses Rs 20,000 a year just by not filing, and that is on one deposit. If he also earns dividends and capital gains, the gap grows. Filing his return, even with help from a tax adviser, would likely cost far less than the extra tax.

## Try It

Do a real-life check this week. Use FBR's **Active Taxpayer Status** service on fbr.gov.pk with your CNIC (or a parent's, with their permission) to see whether you are on the ATL. Then open Igent's **Settings** and make sure **Tax Status** is set correctly to Filer or Non-Filer, so Igent's after-tax comparisons match your real situation.

## Common Mistakes

- Thinking an NTN alone makes you a filer. You must file a return.
- Comparing investments before tax instead of after tax.
- Forgetting to file one year and silently going back to non-filer rates.
- Leaving investments out of the wealth statement.
- Quoting a tax rate from memory or an old article. Rates change every budget.

## Key Takeaways

- A filer is someone on FBR's Active Taxpayers List, earned by filing a return each year.
- Withholding tax is cut at the source from profit and dividends.
- Capital gains tax applies to the profit when you sell, not the whole amount.
- Non-filers often pay about double, so filing can mean much higher returns.
- Check current rates on fbr.gov.pk or ask Igent; never assume last year's.
