## The Big Idea

A portfolio is not a pile of things you bought. It is a plan: what share of your money goes into each kind of asset, and why. Once the plan exists, your job is mostly to keep it on track. That is called **rebalancing**, and it quietly makes you buy low and sell high.

## Explain It Like I'm New

### Start with target weights

A **target weight** is the share of your portfolio you want in each part. For example: 50% equity funds, 40% income and money market funds, 10% gold. These are your **asset allocation**.

How do you choose? Go back to two ideas from earlier lessons:

- **Time horizon:** money needed within a year or two does not belong in stocks.
- **Risk capacity and tolerance:** how much loss you can afford, and how much you can stomach without panic selling. Use the lower of the two.

### Core and satellite

A simple, strong structure:

- **Core (most of your money, say 70% to 90%):** diversified, low-maintenance holdings. Money market and income funds, National Savings, index or equity funds, VPS for retirement.
- **Satellite (a small part, say 10% to 30%):** your own picks. Single stocks, sector bets, extra gold.

The core does the heavy lifting. The satellite is where you use your thesis-writing and sizing skills. If a satellite idea fails, it should hurt, not ruin. Beginners can keep the satellite at zero until they have practised.

### Life stages (examples, not rules)

Your mix should change as your life changes. These are illustrations only, not advice for you:

| Stage (example) | Possible mix | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Young, stable job, no dependants | Equity-heavy, e.g. 70% growth / 30% safe | Long horizon, time to recover |
| Family, school fees in 5-10 years | Balanced, e.g. 50 / 50 | Growth, but some goals are near |
| Near retirement | Safety-heavy, e.g. 30 / 70 | Need income and stability |
| Retired | Mostly income and safe options | Cannot wait out long falls |

A goal with a date (a wedding in 2 years, a car next year) should be moved into safer options as the date gets closer.

### Why portfolios drift

Suppose stocks have a great year. Your 50% equity becomes 60% without you doing anything. You are now taking more risk than you planned, right after prices went up. Drift is natural. Rebalancing fixes it.

### Two ways to rebalance

| Method | How it works | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Calendar** | Check once or twice a year on a fixed date and move back to target | Simple, low effort |
| **Bands** | Act only when a part drifts beyond a set range, e.g. 5 percentage points from target | Fewer, more meaningful trades |

Many people combine them: look on a fixed date, act only if a band is broken.

### The cheapest way to rebalance

Selling has costs. On PSX you pay brokerage and other charges, and selling at a gain can trigger **capital gains tax (CGT)**. Fund redemptions can also have CGT and sometimes a back-end load. Tax rules change with each budget, so check current rates before you act.

So try these first, in order:

1. **Point new money** (salary savings, bonuses) at the underweight part.
2. **Redirect dividends** and fund payouts to the underweight part.
3. **Sell only what is needed**, and only when the drift is beyond your band.


## A Pakistani Example

*All numbers are made-up round figures for teaching, not real data or advice.*

Hina, 35, has Rs 1,000,000 to invest for at least 7 years. Her emergency fund is separate. Her plan:

| Part | Target | Starting amount |
|---|---|---|
| Equity funds (core growth) | 50% | Rs 500,000 |
| Income and money market funds (core safe) | 40% | Rs 400,000 |
| Gold | 10% | Rs 100,000 |

Her band is 5 percentage points. One year later, after a strong stock market, her holdings are:

| Part | Value now | Weight now | Target value (of Rs 1,200,000) | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Equity funds | Rs 680,000 | 56.7% | Rs 600,000 | Rs 80,000 too much |
| Income and money market | Rs 430,000 | 35.8% | Rs 480,000 | Rs 50,000 too little |
| Gold | Rs 90,000 | 7.5% | Rs 120,000 | Rs 30,000 too little |
| **Total** | **Rs 1,200,000** | 100% | | |

Equity is 6.7 points over target, beyond her 5-point band. She has two choices:

- **Option A, sell:** redeem Rs 80,000 of equity funds and move Rs 50,000 to income funds and Rs 30,000 to gold. Quick, but there may be CGT on the gains and some costs.
- **Option B, new money:** Hina saves about Rs 25,000 a month. For the next few months she sends all of it to income funds and gold, and sells nothing. Slower, but no selling costs or tax.

She chooses a mix: she redeems Rs 40,000 of equity now and directs new savings to the underweight parts. Notice what rebalancing did: it made her trim what had gone up and add to what had lagged, without any prediction.

## Common Mistakes

- **No targets at all.** Buying whatever looks good leads to an accidental, lopsided portfolio.
- **Rebalancing too often.** Small drifts are fine. Trading every month mainly feeds costs and tax.
- **Never rebalancing.** Letting winners grow until one asset decides your whole future.
- **Ignoring tax and costs.** Selling when new money could have done the job.
- **A satellite that ate the core.** If your "small" stock picks are half your money, they are not a satellite.

## Key Takeaways

- Set target weights from your horizon and the lower of your risk capacity and tolerance.
- Core and satellite: a big diversified core, a small part for your own ideas.
- Rebalance by calendar, by bands, or both.
- Use new money and dividends first. Sell only when drift is beyond your band.
- Shift towards safety as goals get close and as life stages change.
