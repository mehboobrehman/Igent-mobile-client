## The Big Idea

Most investing losses do not come from bad luck. They come from our own brains, and from people who know how to exploit them. Fear makes us sell at the bottom. Greed makes us buy at the top. Scammers use both. The good news: once you can name a trap, it gets much easier to step around it.

## Explain It Like I'm New

### The four big mental traps

**FOMO** (fear of missing out): a stock doubles, everyone is talking about it, and you buy near the top because you cannot bear to miss out. The easy gain is already gone.
*Rule:* decide on value and your reasons, not on recent gains. If you still want in, buy in small steps.

**Panic selling:** the market falls sharply on scary news and you sell everything. A paper loss becomes a real one, and you often miss the recovery.
*Rule:* money you need soon should not be in shares at all. For long-term money, re-check your reasons for owning, not the price.

**Anchoring:** fixing on a number that does not matter. "I bought at Rs 200, so I will only sell at Rs 200." The market does not know or care what you paid.
*Rule:* ask, "Would I buy this today at this price?" If not, your reason for holding may be gone.

**Overconfidence:** a few lucky wins make you feel like an expert. You trade more, take bigger positions and borrow. Every trade has costs and taxes, and they quietly eat returns.
*Rule:* fewer, better decisions. Never borrow to buy shares.

Two cousins worth knowing: **loss aversion** (holding losers forever to avoid admitting a loss) and **recency bias** (assuming last year's winners will win again).

### Tips groups and pump-and-dumps

You get invited to a "VIP" WhatsApp or Telegram group. An "expert" posts a "sure-shot" target for a small, cheap stock. The price starts rising. Members pile in.

This is often a **pump-and-dump**: organisers buy a thinly traded stock cheaply, hype it, then sell to the people rushing in. When they stop buying, the price collapses, and late buyers are left holding losses.

Market manipulation, false trading and spreading misleading information to move prices are offences under the **Securities Act, 2015**. Giving investment advice for a fee needs an SECP licence. Joining in can leave you with losses, or even awkward questions yourself.

Warning signs:
- Small stock, low volume, sudden spike with no official news.
- Urgency: "buy before it's too late!"
- "Guaranteed" targets.
- Paid membership for "calls".

### Ponzi and "double your money" schemes

A **Ponzi scheme** pays early investors with money from new investors, not from real profits. It works until new money slows, then collapses. Most people lose most of their money.

Red flags:
- "Fixed monthly profit" far above bank rates, with "no risk".
- "Double your money in a year."
- Rewards for bringing in new members (pyramid or MLM style).
- Pressure to reinvest instead of withdrawing.
- A company registration certificate waved as proof. Being registered as a company does **not** allow a firm to take deposits from the public. Taking deposits without a licence is illegal.

Simple test: **if it beats the safe rate by a lot and says "no risk", walk away.** Real investments that can earn more can also lose.

### Unlicensed brokers and fake apps

Some "brokers" are not licensed in Pakistan. Others are fake apps copying a real firm's name and logo. Offshore forex, CFD and "trading" apps not licensed in Pakistan are risky: if they freeze your money or disappear, no Pakistani regulator can help you get it back.

**How to check a licence:**
- Stock brokers: check the broker lists on the PSX and SECP websites.
- Mutual funds and AMCs: check MUFAP and SECP.
- Banks: check SBP.
- Download apps only from links on the firm's official website.
- Pay only into the firm's official account, from your own bank account. Never pay cash to an agent or into a personal account.

### How to complain

1. Write down dates, amounts, names and reference numbers.
2. Complain to the firm in writing and get a complaint reference number.
3. If it is not resolved, escalate. Broker problems go to PSX's investor complaint channel and SECP's online complaint system. AMC, VPS and insurance problems go to SECP. Bank problems go to SBP or the Banking Mohtasib.
4. For online fraud, contact your bank immediately and use the federal cybercrime complaint portal.
5. Beware "recovery agents" who promise to get your money back for a fee. That is often a second scam.

Find the official complaint portals through each regulator's homepage, since links change.

## A Pakistani Example

Made-up numbers. A scheme promises **5% profit every month**. Sounds modest? That is **60% a year** in simple terms, and about **80% a year** if reinvested (1.05 multiplied by itself 12 times ≈ 1.80). Compare that with an example safe rate of 10% a year. Something offering six to eight times the safe rate "with no risk" is almost certainly paying old investors with new money.

A pump-and-dump in numbers. Ali buys **10,000 shares at Rs 20** (Rs 200,000) after a "VIP call", near the top of a spike. The organisers sell. The price falls to **Rs 10**. Ali's holding is now Rs 100,000, a **50% loss**. To get back to Rs 200,000, the share must **double**, a 100% gain. Losses hurt more than they look.

## Common Mistakes

- Buying because a stock "is going up and everyone is buying".
- Selling everything in a crash with money meant for the long term.
- Holding a broken investment just to "get back to even".
- Trusting a tips group, a friend's "inside news" or a "guaranteed" return.
- Sending money to a personal account or an app you found on social media.

## Key Takeaways

- Name the trap: FOMO, panic, anchoring, overconfidence.
- Pump-and-dumps target small, thin stocks through tips groups.
- High "guaranteed" returns with "no risk" are a Ponzi red flag.
- Use only licensed firms; verify on PSX, SECP, MUFAP or SBP websites.
- Complain to the firm first in writing, then escalate to the regulator.
