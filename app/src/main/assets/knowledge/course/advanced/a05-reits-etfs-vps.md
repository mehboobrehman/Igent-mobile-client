## The Big Idea

Beyond single shares and ordinary mutual funds, Pakistan offers three useful "packaged" products. A **REIT** lets you own a slice of income-producing property. An **ETF** lets you buy a whole index in one trade on PSX. A **VPS pension fund** helps you save for retirement with a tax credit on the way in. Each has its own pricing quirks. Knowing them saves you from paying too much or locking money away by mistake.

## Explain It Like I'm New

### REITs: property without buying a plot

A **REIT** (Real Estate Investment Trust) is a **closed-end** trust that owns real estate. SECP regulates REITs. You buy **units** on PSX through your broker, just like shares.

**Closed-end** means you cannot hand your units back to the manager for cash. To exit, you sell to another investor at the market price.

There are three types:

| Type | Earns from | Income pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Rental REIT | Rent from malls, offices, etc. | Steady, if buildings stay let |
| Developmental REIT | Building and selling projects | Lumpy; may pay little for years |
| Hybrid REIT | A mix | Read the offering document |

**NAV per unit** (net asset value) = (property value + cash + other assets − liabilities) ÷ units. An independent valuer values the property regularly.

**Price vs NAV:** a REIT's market price often sits below NAV. That gap is the **discount** = (NAV − price) ÷ NAV. Why? You cannot redeem at NAV, trading is often thin, investors may doubt the valuation, and high safe rates make property yields look less exciting. A discount is not automatically a bargain. Many discounts last for years.

**Distribution yield** (the REIT version of dividend yield) = distributions per unit over 12 months ÷ market price. Use the market price, not face value. Remember that a rise in NAV from revaluation is not cash, so it does not mean a bigger payout. Rental REITs usually pay out most of their income, because tax rules link their exemption to paying out a minimum share of it. Check current rules.

### ETFs: an index in one click

An **ETF** (Exchange-Traded Fund) is an **open-end** index fund whose units trade on PSX like shares. It tries to copy an index, for example a basket of large KSE-100 stocks or a Shariah index.

- You buy through your broker and hold units in your CDC account.
- The fund has a NAV, but the **market price** can trade at a small **premium** (above NAV) or **discount** (below NAV), especially when trading is thin.
- **Tracking error** is how far the ETF's return drifts from its index, due to fees and cash.
- Many PSX ETFs trade low volumes with wide gaps between buy and sell quotes. Always use a **limit order** (you set the maximum price you will pay).

ETFs suit low-cost, rules-based, long-term equity exposure. For larger sums, an AMC index fund that you buy and redeem directly at NAV may be easier.

### VPS: your own pension pot

The **Voluntary Pension System (VPS)** is a private retirement scheme regulated by SECP. You open an **individual pension account** with a pension fund manager (usually an AMC). Your money goes into **sub-funds**: equity, debt and money market. You choose an **allocation scheme** (for example aggressive, balanced or conservative) that sets the mix. Shariah-compliant versions exist.

**The tax credit concept:** under the Income Tax Ordinance, an eligible taxpayer can get a tax credit on VPS contributions. The credit works at your **average tax rate**, on contributions up to a cap linked to your income. In simple terms: contribution × your average tax rate ≈ tax saved, within the limit. Salaried people usually give their VPS statement to their employer's payroll. Caps and conditions change in Finance Acts, so check FBR or your pension fund manager before relying on any numbers.

**The catch:** the money is meant for retirement. Withdrawing early is taxed in a way that can claw back the credit. So VPS is a poor place for an emergency fund.

### When each fits

| Product | Fits when you want | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| REIT | Rental-style income without buying property | Discount to NAV, thin trading, rate sensitivity |
| ETF | Cheap, simple index exposure through PSX | Low volume, wide spreads, concentration |
| VPS | Long-term retirement saving, with tax benefit if you pay income tax | Money locked until retirement |

This is general education. Which product suits you depends on your goals, taxes and timeline.

## A Pakistani Example

Made-up numbers for an imaginary rental REIT, "Karachi Mall REIT":

- NAV per unit: **Rs 30**. Market price: **Rs 21**.
- Discount = (30 − 21) ÷ 30 = **30%**.
- Distributions over the last year: **Rs 2.10** per unit.
- Distribution yield on price = 2.10 ÷ 21 = **10%**. (On NAV it would be only 7%.)

Now compare with a safe option. Suppose a money market fund yields 9% before tax (an example, not today's rate). The REIT's 10% before tax is only slightly higher, but it carries property risk, price swings and thin trading. You would want a clear extra margin, or real rent growth, before choosing the REIT.

VPS example, also made up: you contribute **Rs 120,000** in a year and your average tax rate is **10%**. Your tax credit could be about 120,000 × 10% = **Rs 12,000**, if you are within the cap and meet the conditions.

## Common Mistakes

- Thinking a big discount to NAV guarantees a profit.
- Comparing a developmental REIT's yield with a rental REIT's yield.
- Using a market order on a thinly traded ETF and overpaying.
- Putting emergency money in VPS, then paying tax to withdraw it early.
- Quoting a VPS tax cap or REIT tax rate from memory. These change.

## Key Takeaways

- REITs are closed-end and trade on PSX, often at a discount to NAV.
- Judge REITs on cash distribution yield at market price, not on revaluation gains.
- ETFs copy an index; check NAV and use limit orders.
- VPS offers a tax credit at your average tax rate, but locks money until retirement.
- Always compare after-tax returns with the safe rate.
