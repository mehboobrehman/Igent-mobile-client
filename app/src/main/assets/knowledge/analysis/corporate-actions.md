# Corporate actions on PSX

Corporate actions change what a shareholder owns or receives. They are announced on the PSX Data Portal under company announcements (query_announcements). The company's own notice always governs dates and terms. General education only, not personal investment advice. Check current rules.

## Board meetings and results
- Companies announce a board meeting date in advance. The meeting approves results and any dividend, bonus or rights.
- The outcome is filed on PSX the same day, often during market hours. Prices can move sharply.
- See analysis/stock-valuation for reading results.

## Cash dividends
**Declaration.** Interim dividends are declared by the board. Final dividends are recommended by the board and approved by shareholders at the AGM.

**Entitlement (book closure).** The notice says the company's share transfer books will be closed for a period. Shareholders on the register at close of business on the day before closure (the cut-off) get the dividend. This works like a record date.

**Settlement matters.** You are on the register only after your purchase settles in CDC. So you must buy early enough for settlement to finish by the cut-off. The last day to buy depends on the PSX settlement cycle (see basics/psx for the current cycle). Buying after that point is "ex-dividend".

**Price adjustment.** On the ex-date the price usually falls by about the dividend. A dividend is not free money. Value moves from the share price to your bank account, minus tax.

**Tax.** Dividend tax is withheld at source before payment. Rates depend on filer status and dividend type (basics/tax). Zakat may also be deducted unless exempted (legal/inheritance-nomination-zakat).

**Payment.** Under section 242 of the Companies Act 2017, listed companies must pay cash dividends electronically into the shareholder's bank account. Shareholders must register an IBAN. For CDC holdings this is done through the broker or CDC Investor Account Services. Companies may withhold dividends where bank details are missing.

**Unclaimed dividends.** Under section 244, dividends unpaid for three years or more, and unclaimed shares, vest in the Federal Government after a final notice period. Keep CDC and IBAN details updated.

## Bonus shares
- Free extra shares in proportion to holding, e.g. a 10% bonus gives 1 share per 10 held.
- The price adjusts down on the ex-date. Total value does not change by itself.
- Adjust past EPS and price history for bonuses when comparing.
- Tax treatment of bonus shares has changed over the years. Check basics/tax and current law.
- Some companies issue bonus instead of cash to keep cash. Ask why.

## Rights issues
- An offer to existing shareholders to buy new shares, usually at a discount, in proportion to holdings.
- **Entitlement** is fixed by a book closure, like dividends.
- **Letters of right** are credited to CDC accounts. **Unpaid rights** trade on PSX for a limited window before subscription closes.
- Choices: subscribe and pay, sell (renounce) the rights on PSX, or do nothing. If you do nothing, rights lapse and your stake is diluted.
- **Subscribing:** ask your broker (CDC participant) or CDC Investor Account Services to submit the subscription and pay by the last date. Rights in a subscription request are blocked from trading.
- **Deep discount:** the share price adjusts down ex-rights. Value only holds if you subscribe or sell the rights. A deep discount mainly makes sure the issue gets taken up. It is not a gift.
- Ask: is the money for growth (good) or to repay debt or cover losses (warning)? Is the issue underwritten? Are sponsors subscribing?

Example (made-up numbers): price Rs 100, 1 right per 4 shares at Rs 60. Theoretical ex-rights price = (4 x 100 + 60) / 5 = Rs 92.

## IPOs and book building
- New listings use a prospectus approved by SECP and PSX. Read it: use of proceeds, sponsor track record, risks, valuation and any lock-in of sponsor shares.
- **Book building:** institutions and high-net-worth investors bid within a price band. The result sets the strike price. The rest is offered to the general public at that price.
- **Retail application:** through PSX's E-IPO system, which links investors, brokers, banks, share registrars and CDC, with online payment through banks. CDC has also run a centralised e-IPO system (CES). Check the prospectus for the channels used.
- Allotment can be partial if oversubscribed. Refunds follow.
- Watch listing-day moves. Thinly floated IPOs can be very volatile.

## Buybacks
- Governed by SECP's Listed Companies (Buy-back of Shares) Regulations. They need a special resolution of shareholders and a public announcement.
- Conditions include a listing track record, not being on the default counter, and keeping minimum free float after purchase.
- In purchases through the exchange, the company reports shares bought and prices daily. Bought shares are held blocked and later cancelled or sold as treasury shares.
- Signal: management thinks shares are cheap, or sponsors want a higher stake. A buyback also reduces free float and liquidity.

## Delisting
- **Voluntary delisting:** sponsors apply under PSX's voluntary delisting regulations. They must offer to buy minority shares at a purchase price, through a purchase agent, within a set window. The offer can stay open for some time after delisting.
- After delisting, shares are hard to sell and information becomes scarce. Minorities should usually decide during the offer.
- Minority holders have at times objected that the offer price was too low.
- **Compulsory delisting:** PSX can delist for persistent non-compliance. Shares may become close to worthless for minorities.

## Mergers and schemes of arrangement
- Amalgamations, demergers and capital restructurings usually run through a scheme of arrangement under sections 279 to 285 of the Companies Act 2017, sanctioned by the High Court (or as the law provides).
- Shareholders vote at court-ordered meetings. A **swap ratio** sets how many new shares you get per old share.
- Check whether the swap ratio is fair versus market prices and book values. Competition clearance may also be needed.

## Opportunities and risks for idea cards
| Action | Possible opportunity | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| Cash dividend | Steady income; high yield stocks | Dividend cut; price falls ex-date; tax |
| Bonus | None by itself | Mistaking it for new value |
| Rights | Buying at discount if business is sound | Dilution; funding losses |
| IPO | Discount pricing at listing | Overvaluation; low float volatility |
| Buyback | Price support; signal of value | Lower liquidity |
| Voluntary delisting | Exit offer above market | Low offer; being stuck after delisting |
| Merger | Value unlock; synergies | Unfair swap ratio |

Rules for the agent: never recommend buying just before book closure for the dividend alone. Always use adjusted prices. Compare total expected return with the after-tax safe rate (savings/safe-rates).
