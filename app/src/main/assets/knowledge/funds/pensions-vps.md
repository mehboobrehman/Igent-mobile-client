# Voluntary Pension System (VPS)

The **Voluntary Pension System** is a private retirement savings scheme regulated by SECP under the **Voluntary Pension System Rules, 2005**. You put money into your own **individual pension account** with a pension fund. It stays invested until the retirement age you choose. Contributions can earn an income tax credit.

VPS suits salaried and self-employed people who pay income tax and want to save for old age. Contributions can come from you, or from your employer on your behalf.

Background on fund categories: funds/fund-types. How AMCs, trustees and statements work: funds/amcs.

## Who runs VPS funds
- **Pension fund managers (PFMs)** licensed by SECP. The Rules allowed asset management companies and life insurance companies to be PFMs. In practice most VPS funds are run by AMCs.
- Each pension fund has a **trustee** (often CDC) that holds the assets. Contributions are paid to the trustee's account for the fund, not to the PFM or an agent.
- MUFAP publishes daily NAVs and returns for VPS sub-funds (see funds/mufap).

## Sub-funds
A pension fund is split into sub-funds. Each issues its own units with its own NAV.
| Sub-fund | Invests in | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Equity | Listed shares | High |
| Debt | Government securities, TFCs/sukuk, bank placements | Low–medium |
| Money market | Short-term government paper and bank deposits | Low |

Islamic pension funds offer **Shariah-compliant** versions (equity, debt, money market) under a Shariah adviser (see shariah/screening).

## Allocation schemes and lifecycle plans
- You pick an **allocation scheme** that splits your money across sub-funds. Typical names are aggressive, balanced, conservative and very conservative. Higher equity share means higher expected return and bigger swings.
- Some PFMs also offer **lifecycle plans**. These move money from equity towards debt and money market as you get closer to retirement.
- If you do not choose, the PFM applies a default (lower-risk) scheme.
- The PFM **rebalances** your sub-fund mix back to the chosen scheme from time to time.
- You can change your scheme, within limits on how often set by the Rules and the offering document.

## Tax credit on contributions (concept)
- **Section 63 of the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001** gives an eligible individual a tax credit for contributions to an approved pension fund under the VPS Rules.
- The credit works at your **average rate of tax**, on contributions up to a cap linked to your taxable income. So the benefit is larger for people with a higher average tax rate.
- Salaried people usually give their VPS statement to their employer's payroll/HR so less tax is deducted. Others claim it in their annual return.
- The cap, eligibility, holding conditions and other details are set by law and can change in any Finance Act. Check FBR, the current Income Tax Ordinance, or the PFM's tax page before quoting numbers. See basics/tax.

## At retirement
You choose a retirement age within the range the Rules allow (the 2005 Rules set it between 60 and 70). Disability can allow earlier retirement. At retirement your units are redeemed and you choose from:
1. **Lump sum:** take part of the balance as cash. A share can be tax-free under the tax law. The allowed cash share has changed over the years, so check the current rules and the tax law before relying on any figure.
2. **Income payment plan:** keep the rest with a PFM and draw monthly instalments under an SECP-approved plan, up to an age set in the Rules.
3. **Annuity:** use the rest to buy an approved annuity (lifetime income) from a life insurance or takaful company. The trustee pays the insurer directly.

When an income payment plan ends, any remaining balance generally goes into an annuity. Check your fund's offering document for its exact options.

## Early withdrawal
- Money is meant to stay until retirement. Withdrawals before retirement age are taxed under the Income Tax Ordinance.
- AMC guides describe this tax as based on your average tax rate over earlier years. In effect it can claw back the tax credit you got.
- So VPS is a poor place for an emergency fund. Keep that in a money market fund (see savings/safe-rates).

## Changing pension fund manager
- You can move all or part of your pension account to another PFM.
- Your units are redeemed at NAV and the money goes directly to the new pension fund, where new units are bought under your chosen scheme.
- The Rules set timing and notice requirements, and offering documents say no transfer charge applies. Check the current rules and both PFMs' terms.

## Nominations and death before retirement
- You fill a **nomination form** when opening the account, naming beneficiaries, their CNICs and shares.
- If you die before retirement, the balance is split among nominees. Each can withdraw (subject to tax rules), move it to their own pension account, or buy an annuity.
- Without a nomination, a succession certificate is usually needed. Keep nominations up to date.

## Employer-linked contributions
- Employers can register employees and contribute to their individual pension accounts, alongside or instead of employee contributions.
- The individual pension account is held in the employee's own name.
- Ask HR whether the employer offers VPS and how its contribution works.

## VPS vs mutual funds
| Point | VPS pension fund | Ordinary mutual fund |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Retirement | Any goal |
| Access to money | Locked until retirement; early exit taxed | Redeem any working day |
| Tax on entry | Tax credit on contributions (s.63) | No credit |
| Tax on exit | Retirement rules; early withdrawal taxed | CGT on gains, tax on dividends |
| Structure | Sub-funds + allocation scheme | Single fund per category |
| Switching | Scheme change and PFM transfer within rules | Conversion within same AMC |
| At the end | Lump sum + income plan or annuity | Cash |

Rule of thumb: use VPS for long-term retirement money you will not touch, especially if you pay income tax. Use mutual funds for goals you may need before retirement. Steps for buying funds are in guides/mutual-funds-step-by-step.

## Checks before investing
- PFM licensed by SECP, fund listed on MUFAP.
- Compare sub-fund returns over several years within the same type (conventional vs Islamic).
- Read the offering document for fees, loads and retirement options.
- Keep every contribution receipt and annual statement for tax claims.

Pension and tax rules change. Confirm with SECP, FBR, your PFM's latest documents, or a licensed adviser. This note is general information, not legal or tax advice.
