# Personal finance basics

> General education, not personal financial advice.

Investing works only on top of a stable money routine. This note covers the routine. The order of steps is in guides/start-here.

## Budgeting
A budget is a plan for where each month's income goes. Keep it simple.

**Example method: 50/30/20.** One popular split is:
- 50% on needs (rent, food, bills, school fees, loan instalments),
- 30% on wants (eating out, shopping, trips),
- 20% on saving and paying off debt.

This is an example, not a rule. A family with high rent or many dependants may only manage 5 to 10% saving at first. That is fine. The habit matters more than the number.

**Other simple methods:**
- **Envelope method:** split cash or sub-accounts by category. When a category is empty, stop.
- **Pay-yourself-first:** decide the saving amount first, spend the rest freely.

## Tracking spending
- Track for one or two months before setting a budget. Most people underestimate small daily spending.
- Use bank and wallet statements, or a simple notebook or spreadsheet.
- Watch large yearly costs too: school admissions, insurance, Eid, weddings, car repair. Divide each by 12 and save for it monthly.

## Pay yourself first and invest automatically
- Move savings out on salary day, before spending starts.
- Set a standing instruction or auto-debit into a savings account or a fund plan. Many AMCs offer monthly investment plans (SIPs). See funds/amcs.
- Automatic saving removes willpower and market timing from the decision.
- Raise the amount when income rises.

## Emergency fund
- Keep **several months of essential expenses** somewhere safe and quick to reach.
- Keep more if income is unstable, you are the only earner, or you have dependants.
- Where to keep it and why: guides/start-here.

## Debt
Debt is not always bad. Expensive or unplanned debt is.

| Type | Key point |
|---|---|
| Credit card | Interest on unpaid balances is usually among the most expensive borrowing. Paying only the minimum keeps you in debt for a long time. Pay the full statement balance each month. |
| Personal loan | Unsecured, so usually costly. Check total cost, processing fee and early repayment charges. |
| Car finance | Often a floating rate. Budget for insurance, fuel and maintenance too. |
| Home finance | Long term, so small rate changes matter a lot. Check the rate type and reset terms. |
| Buy now, pay later (BNPL) | Feels free, but late fees add up and many small plans become a big monthly burden. Count them as debt. |
| Informal borrowing | From family, friends or lenders. No clear terms can damage relationships. Private moneylenders can charge very high rates. Write down terms. |

### Floating rates and KIBOR, in concept
- KIBOR (Karachi Interbank Offered Rate) is a benchmark for rupee lending between banks. SBP publishes the rates.
- Many bank loans use a floating rate: **benchmark (such as KIBOR) plus a fixed spread**, reset at set intervals.
- When the benchmark rises, your instalment or tenor rises at the next reset. When it falls, it eases.
- Your offer letter states the benchmark, spread, reset dates and charges. Read it before signing. Ask for a payment schedule under a higher-rate scenario.
- Islamic finance products use their own structures, but pricing is often linked to a benchmark too. Ask the bank.

### Pay off expensive debt before investing
- Paying off a loan earns a guaranteed return equal to its rate.
- If a loan costs more than the safe rate after tax (savings/safe-rates), pay it down first.
- Order: highest-rate debt first, while paying the minimum on others.
- Never borrow to invest in stocks.

## Goal planning in PKR with inflation
Common goals: Hajj or Umrah, children's education, marriage, a house deposit, retirement.

**Step 1: future cost.** Prices rise, so today's cost is too low.

> Future cost = Today's cost x (1 + inflation)^years

**Step 2: monthly saving needed.**

> Monthly saving = Future cost x r / ((1 + r)^n - 1)
> where r = expected monthly return after tax (yearly return / 12, roughly) and n = number of months.

**Worked example (made-up numbers):**
- Goal: a child's university fund in 10 years. Today's cost Rs 1,000,000.
- Assumed inflation: 10% a year. Future cost = 1,000,000 x 1.10^10 = about **Rs 2,594,000**.
- Assumed return after tax: 12% a year, so r = 0.01 a month. n = 120 months.
- (1.01)^120 = about 3.30. Monthly saving = 2,594,000 x 0.01 / 2.30 = about **Rs 11,280 a month**.
- If you already have savings for this goal, the monthly amount falls.

Tips:
- Education and medical costs often rise faster than general inflation. Use a higher assumption for them.
- Use the live CPI as a starting point (macro/inflation). Igent can run the numbers in chat.
- Review the plan every year and adjust.

## Match each goal to instruments
The time to the goal decides the product:
- Under 1 year (for example Umrah next year): bank savings, money market funds, short NSS.
- 1 to 3 years: income funds, NSS certificates, term deposits.
- 5+ years (education, retirement): equity or index funds alongside fixed income. VPS for retirement (funds/pensions-vps).
- Move money to safer options as the goal date gets close.

Full table and sizing: portfolio/risk-and-sizing.

## Records and documents
- Keep a folder (paper and a backup) with: CNIC copies, bank and fund statements, broker and CDC statements, NSS certificates, tax returns and withholding certificates, loan papers, insurance policies, property papers.
- Keep tax documents for several years in case of questions.
- Never store passwords or PINs with these records.
- Igent's My Money can hold a summary of what you own. See guides/complaints-and-safety for records to keep.

## Family discussions and nominees
- Talk with your spouse or family about income, debts, goals and where documents are.
- Name a nominee on each account, fund and certificate, and keep it updated.
- A nominee does not decide who inherits. See legal/inheritance-nomination-zakat.

## Zakat planning
- Zakat may be deducted at source on some savings and investments at the start of Ramadan, unless a valid exemption declaration is filed.
- Plan cash flow for it, and ask a qualified scholar how to calculate zakat on assets not deducted at source.
- Details: legal/inheritance-nomination-zakat.
